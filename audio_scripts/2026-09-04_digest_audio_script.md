Welcome to the September fourth Neuro Daily at Cabbageland!

Today is a myelination is computation, not cable management day.

The main keep is directly relevant, titled, Axonal delay dispersion decides whether a neuron detects an event or a sequence, and predicts cortical column diameter.

It wins because it takes an old delay-line intuition and turns it into a sharper mechanistic claim with measurable knobs. The usual vague story is that conduction delays can matter for temporal coding. Fine. This paper asks the more useful question: what anatomical variable decides whether a downstream neuron behaves like a plain event detector or like an order-selective sequence detector? Its answer is the dispersion of the incoming axonal delays. That is a much better claim because it cashes out into something an anatomist could in principle measure.

The central simulation result is clean. With narrow delay dispersion, the model population mostly behaves like event detectors. Sequence detectors are basically absent. As the dispersion widens, sequence detectors emerge and eventually overtake event detectors. The crossover point tracks the interval between the two events with a slope reported at zero point nine two, with a confidence interval from zero point eight three to one point zero one. In plainer language, once the delay spread gets close to the temporal gap between events, the circuit flips into a sequence-reading regime.

The reversed-order control also helps the paper. Across the main operating range, one hundred ninety of one hundred ninety-two classified sequence detectors stay silent to the reversed sequence. That means the result is not just broad conjunctive activation with a prettier name. The model is actually detecting order.

The better result comes next. The same dispersion that buys sequence selectivity also sets the code's limits. The maximum codable interval tracks the available delay range, which means anatomy sets a hard upper bound on how far apart the events can be and still be matched. The tolerated timing error also stays harshly small, around zero point eight two milliseconds at the stricter threshold and about one point four six milliseconds at the looser one. That is much more useful than generic rhetoric about precise timing. It says the code only works inside a very specific precision budget.

The boldest claim is the cortical-column one. The paper argues that column diameter should scale with horizontal conduction velocity times the effective coincidence window. At the reference velocity they use, the predicted diameter band is about three hundred to six hundred micrometers. They then note that the two areas with the needed direct measurements are at least compatible with that relation. That is not proof, and the paper is honest that two points are not a regression. But it is still a real prediction instead of a decorative story about cortex geometry.

The best existing archive anchor for today's keep is titled, A Whole-Brain Dynamical Framework Linking Resting-State Activity to TMS-Evoked Responses. That paper matters here because it makes the intervention-facing version of the same argument. Timing structure is only interesting if it changes how perturbations propagate through a dynamical system.

The second anchor is titled, Temporally constraining source imaging estimates in an underdetermined neural system with eigenmodes of cortical geometry. It belongs here because it also punishes an overly elegant temporal story. In that paper, adding the wrong temporal structure to a good spatial prior does not help. In today's paper, delay dispersion only matters if the anatomy and the coincidence window really support the code you say exists.

The third anchor is titled, Causal connectivity maps derived from single-pulse interleaved TMS slash functional MRI. It matters because any theory about delay-based temporal readout that hopes to matter for intervention still has to survive contact with actual perturbational downstream circuit measurements.

The fourth anchor is the windowed-detrending TMS-EEG artifact paper. It belongs here for the simple reason that millisecond theory is wasted if the sensing layer is dirty enough to hallucinate temporal structure.

The fresh clinical and translational papers were worth checking but did not beat the keep. The first was titled, Neurophysiological signatures of Stanford Neuromodulation Therapy in treatment resistant depression. It is interesting because the randomized sham-controlled TMS-EEG design is stronger than average, and the active arm shows progressive, site-specific excitability reduction with downstream subgenual cingulate source changes. But the response-prediction part is still exploratory and only twelve patients were in the active group.

The second clinical paper was titled, Multi-target focused ultrasound neuromodulation of a subcallosal cingulate mediated depression network: A case study using personalized functional mapping. The appeal is obvious. It treats depression targeting as a distributed network problem instead of a single magic point problem. But it is still a case study, so it is a signpost, not a preserve.

The current standing-interest checks also stayed below threshold. In the cognitive behavioral therapy plus interventional psychiatry lane, the narrowed current-window search did not surface a stronger new preserve than the archive meta-analysis on noninvasive brain stimulation combined with evidence-based psychotherapy. In the hypnosis and hypnotherapy lane, the same thing happened. No stronger current-window mechanism paper beat the archive note on hypnotic consciousness and ideomotor suggestions. That is fine. The right move is not to force a weak paper into the archive out of housekeeping guilt.

So the useful lesson today is simple. Axonal delay dispersion is not just wiring trivia. In this paper it determines whether neurons behave like event detectors or order-selective sequence detectors, sets the upper bound of the temporal code, and implies a hard millisecond precision budget. That is a better way to talk about timing, myelination, and intervention-relevant temporal structure than the usual mush.
