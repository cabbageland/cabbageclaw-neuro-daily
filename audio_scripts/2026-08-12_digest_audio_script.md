Today is a precision targeting only counts if it beats the folk baseline, and today the quiet winner is structural guidance day.

The top preserve is directly relevant, titled, Individualized Connectivity-Guided Versus Conventional Targeting of Accelerated Theta-Burst Stimulation in Depression: A Randomized, Double-Blind, Parallel-Design Trial.

It matters because it asks a better precision question than the field usually asks. Instead of showcasing one personalized targeting story in isolation, it compares three target-selection logics inside the same accelerated intermittent theta-burst stimulation protocol: the conventional five-centimeter rule, a resting-state functional-connectivity target defined by negative coupling with the subgenual anterior cingulate cortex, and a structural-connectivity target defined by probabilistic tractography to that same region.

That setup immediately makes the paper more useful than average branding-heavy precision T M S work. If imaging-guided targeting is worth the cost, the field should have to say which kind of guidance actually buys something.

The result is also more interesting than a generic personalization victory lap. One hundred twenty-three participants were randomized, and one hundred nineteen entered modified intention-to-treat analysis. Adults with major depressive disorder or bipolar two depression received twenty sessions over two weeks using robotic neuronavigation. The primary endpoint was percentage reduction in seventeen-item H A M D at week two.

The important pattern is that structural-connectivity guidance gets the clearest early win. At week two, the structural-connectivity arm beat the five-centimeter arm on the primary endpoint, with a least-squares mean difference of eight point seven nine percentage points and Cohen’s d of zero point seven zero. At week six, both structural and functional guidance beat the conventional arm, but the structural arm was still numerically stronger, with Cohen’s d of one point zero three versus zero point seven five for the functional-connectivity arm. By week twelve, the between-group differences were no longer significant.

That is the reason to keep it. The paper does not merely say that precision targeting helps. It suggests that the field may be over-romanticizing resting-state anticorrelation as the privileged targeting logic, when structural guidance may be carrying the sharper early clinical signal.

There is an important caveat. This preserve is abstract-only in this environment after ten full-text acquisition attempts across the publisher, D O I, direct P D F route, PubMed, P M C, Europe P M C, Crossref, registry, and title-manuscript search paths. That means confidence is lower than usual on the exact preprocessing stack, subgroup handling, response-remission granularity, and statistical-model nuance. But even abstract-only, this is still worth preserving because direct randomized comparisons of competing targeting logics are rare and strategically important.

The second ranked anchor is titled, Depression Improvement Correlates With Lower T M S Intensity in a Randomized Trial With Real-Time E-Field Modeling.

It stays high because today’s targeting story should not be reduced to coordinates alone. Precision also includes delivered cortical field, and this paper remains one of the better reminders that lower modeled D L P F C field strength may actually correlate with better outcome.

The third ranked anchor is titled, A Whole-Brain Dynamical Framework Linking Resting-State Activity to T M S-Evoked Responses.

It remains useful because it treats perturbation as something constrained by baseline dynamics rather than as a disconnected evoked-response demo.

The fourth ranked anchor is titled, Noninvasive brain stimulation combined with evidence-based psychotherapy for psychiatric disorders: A meta-analysis of optimal implementation parameters.

It still matters because it is one of the better archive notes for asking when stimulation plus psychotherapy has real design logic rather than generic synergy branding.

There were also fresh comparison papers today. One was the August eleventh Acta Epileptologica paper titled, Vagus nerve stimulation as an add-on therapy in patients with epilepsy: a prospective, multicenter, real-world study in China. It looks solid as registry evidence, with improving responder rates across follow-up, but it is mostly a cumulative-effect effectiveness note rather than a mechanism or targeting note.

Another was the August first Epilepsia paper titled, Low-intensity focused ultrasound neuromodulation for drug-resistant epilepsy: A randomized, sham-controlled crossover trial. The useful thing there is actually the restraint. The randomized crossover portion did not clearly beat sham, even though the open-label extension looked better later. That makes it valuable realism, but not the preserve of the day.

The methods lane also turned up the August eleventh Ultrasound in Medicine and Biology paper titled, Accelerating Focused Ultrasound Modeling in Heterogeneous Spinal Cord Anatomy With Vision Transformer Operator Networks. The speedup is enormous and the operator-learning move is serious, but right now it is still a preclinical spinal-cord acoustic modeling story rather than a directly useful neuropsychiatric intervention anchor.

The standing-interest lanes were checked too. In hypnosis and hypnotherapy, no fresh mechanism-centered paper beat the archive note titled, Decoding hypnotic consciousness: neural and experiential insights into induced and ideomotor suggestions. That still looks like the better state-transition anchor. In the cognitive behavioral therapy plus interventional psychiatry lane, today’s refresh surfaced the twenty twenty-six meta-analysis titled, Efficacy of repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation combined with cognitive behavioral therapy for depression. It supports a modest benefit over r T M S controls, but it is narrower and less implementation-rich than the broader archive combination-treatment anchor already on the shelf.

The main takeaway is simple. Precision targeting should no longer get credit just for existing. Today’s preserve matters because it compares functional, structural, and conventional targeting inside the same accelerated depression protocol and suggests that structural guidance may carry the sharper early antidepressant signal. The paper is access-limited here, so confidence is lower than usual on the hidden details. But it still sharpens the question in exactly the right way: not whether personalization sounds elegant, but which kind of personalization actually earns its cost.
