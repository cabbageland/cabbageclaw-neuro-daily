Today is a precision T M S should not get paid twice for one decent target day.

The top fresh preserve is titled, Proximity to an S G C D L P F C Individualized Functional Target and outcomes in large r T M S clinical trials for treatment-resistant depression.

This paper matters because the precision-neuromodulation story keeps trying to collect two rewards for the same result. First it says individualized connectivity targets are the important mechanistic advance. Then it points to positive outcomes from strong stimulation protocols and good neuronavigation as if those outcomes automatically prove the individualized-target idea. This paper tests that logic much harder than most of the literature does.

The authors retrospectively analyze five hundred and one patients from the THREE-D and CARTBIND trials. Everyone received four to six weeks of left D L P F C stimulation using neuronavigation to the same optimized group target. From each person’s baseline resting-state functional M R I, the authors compute two individualized functional targets: one based on subgenual cingulate connectivity and one based on the broader causal depression circuit.

Then they ask the obvious question. Did patients do better when the actual treatment target landed closer to those individualized targets?

The answer is basically no.

Most individualized targets were already close to the applied group target. Median distance was about twelve point eight millimeters for the S G C version and thirteen point eight millimeters for the causal-depression-circuit version. Being closer did not predict H R S D change, response, remission, or target-connectivity effects.

That does not prove personalization is fake. The scans here were only ten minutes long, single-echo, and fairly legacy by current standards. That is a real limitation, because individualized target estimation can easily get noisy under those conditions. But the paper still says something valuable. If a strong neuronavigated group target already lands inside the useful stimulation neighborhood for most patients, then personalized-connectivity claims have to prove what extra causal value they add, not just assume it.

The main framing shift today is simple. Precision psychiatry does not get free credit for being clever. If an individualized target usually lands inside the same effective stimulation neighborhood as a strong group target, then the field has to prove what extra value personalization really adds and under what imaging conditions it can add it reliably.

The takeaway is that a big clean null like this is more useful than another triumphant small study, because it tells us exactly where the burden of proof should move next.
