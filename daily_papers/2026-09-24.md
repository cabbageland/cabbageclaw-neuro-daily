## Short overview

The new preserve is **A randomized double-blind sham-controlled preliminary study of a 5-week protocol of repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation (rTMS) combined with cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) for gambling disorder in Indonesia**.

It is worth keeping because it is one of the few recent full-text randomized tests of active versus sham rTMS added to a defined CBT protocol for a behavioral addiction. The result is not a triumph. Both active and sham rTMS plus CBT improved gambling severity, and active rTMS did not separate on the primary G-SAS symptom trajectory. The interesting residue is narrower: active left-DLPFC rTMS was associated with faster reductions in tonic gambling urge, gambling expectancies, and perceived inability to stop gambling.

That is exactly the kind of result the archive should preserve: a process-signal paper that refuses to become an efficacy fairy tale.

## Most relevant paper

**A randomized double-blind sham-controlled preliminary study of a 5-week protocol of repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation (rTMS) combined with cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) for gambling disorder in Indonesia**

This is the most relevant paper today because it makes the combination-treatment question concrete and then gives an inconvenient answer. Active rTMS plus CBT did not beat sham rTMS plus CBT on the main gambling severity outcome. It did appear to speed improvement in tonic urge and gambling-related cognitions, but in a 27-person study with baseline imbalances on those same secondary measures.

The best use is design discipline. If a future hybrid intervention claims synergy, it should specify the process variable, measure it repeatedly, keep the primary clinical endpoint separate, and resist calling a slope change a treatment win before relapse, behavior, and harm outcomes move.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

- **For CBT plus interventional psychiatry:** today's preserved trial is a cautionary useful hit. It supports the possibility of process acceleration, not a clear active-stimulation efficacy claim.
- **For clinical/translational neuromodulation:** left-DLPFC rTMS remains a plausible craving/control target, but this trial does not prove enough to change clinical practice.
- **For implementation baselines:** the existing NIBS-plus-psychotherapy meta-analysis remains the broader map: modality, timing, therapy fidelity, and human delivery probably matter more than the phrase "combined treatment."
- **For CBT mechanism:** the OCD CBT EEG note stays relevant because it models the right logic: name the practiced process, then measure behavior and physiology around it.
- **For exposure and extinction:** the vmPFC tDCS extinction paper is the stronger physiological anchor for stimulation-as-learning-augmentation, while today's rTMS gambling trial is clinically more direct but mechanistically thinner.
- **For hypnosis and hypnotherapy:** the standing search did not surface a stronger fresh hypnosis or hypnotherapy paper today.
- **For computational/network/control:** a thalamocortical epilepsy state-transition model surfaced, but PMC full text is embargoed until 2027 and publisher full-text access was not enough today for a preserved note. It stays a candidate, not a preserve.
- **For methods/neuroengineering:** the practical baseline is process-specific measurement: craving, cue reactivity, response inhibition, relapse behavior, and state markers should be modeled separately instead of mashed into one treatment score.

## One-paragraph takeaway

September twenty-fourth is about staying unsentimental with hybrid interventions. The new rTMS-plus-CBT gambling-disorder paper is useful because it gives the field a small, real, sham-controlled test and a non-glossy result: gambling severity improved in both groups, active stimulation did not win on the primary outcome, and the more interesting signal lived in faster changes to urge and gambling cognitions. That is not nothing. It is also not a license to say the combination works. The next better study should decide in advance whether it is testing symptom superiority, process acceleration, relapse prevention, or mechanism, then measure that thing hard.
