# 2026-11-25

## Theme

Target engagement needs a local counterfactual, not just a connectivity map.

## Short overview

One new full-text preserve bridges network neuroscience and precision neuroengineering: personalized TMS changes downstream BOLD relative to nearby active control placements, rather than merely producing a familiar-looking activation map. Existing computational and clinical notes supply the missing tests of state dependence and therapeutic benefit. The useful result is target-specific perturbation evidence in healthy adults—not proof that negative connectivity universally identifies the best depression target.

## Ranked list

1. **Target- and network-specific downstream effects of personalized precision transcranial magnetic stimulation: Evidence from concurrent functional neuroimaging**
   Directly relevant network neuroscience, targeting, and neuroengineering; translational rather than clinical efficacy evidence. New full-text preserve; bioRxiv version 1, posted November 24, 2026, not peer reviewed.
   Why it matters: Twenty healthy adults underwent concurrent TMS-fMRI at individualized negative- and positive-connectivity prefrontal targets and nearby active controls. Negative-connectivity target stimulation produced a more negative sgACC BOLD estimate than its control (adjusted difference −1.03, Bonferroni-corrected p = .003). Positive-target comparisons were null, but lower fields and weaker target–control connectivity separation make the sign comparison unequal. The design strengthens the local target-engagement claim, not a general treatment rule.

2. **A personalized map of where, when, and how to stimulate the brain to elicit controlled responses**
   Directly relevant computational neuroscience/control framing; adjacent inspiration for a prospective intervention. Existing bioRxiv preserve surfaced October 8; repository note consulted, not a fresh primary-source or version audit.
   Why it matters: Personalized resting-fMRI surrogate models search target, baseline state, and paired-region stimulation. They supply a state-dependent hypothesis generator, whereas today's new paper supplies an empirical perturbation assay. Resting-state prediction and synthetic BOLD perturbations do not validate real stimulation responses; prospective target-versus-control measurements are exactly the missing bridge.

3. **Task-guided accelerated cTBS simultaneously treats depression and social dysfunction in patients with major depressive disorder: a randomized clinical trial**
   Directly relevant clinical/translational neuromodulation. Existing Neuropsychopharmacology preserve surfaced May 28; repository note consulted, not newly reread primary evidence.
   Why it matters: A task-localized right-DLPFC target is tested against sham in MDD with symptoms, social behavior, and connectivity readouts. The preserved report describes 70 randomized patients and 63 completing treatment plus MRI follow-up. It adds a clinical objective but does not establish that task localization beats alternative targeting, or that an imaging change mediates symptom improvement. A social-computation task is not CBT, and this trial is not evidence for a psychotherapy–stimulation combination.

## Most relevant paper

**Target- and network-specific downstream effects of personalized precision transcranial magnetic stimulation: Evidence from concurrent functional neuroimaging**

Lueckel and colleagues combine individual resting-state connectivity, gyral accessibility, and electric-field optimization, then compare each optimized target with a nearby placement intended to spare that target while matching peripheral experience. This is the right counterfactual for asking whether precision adds anything beyond stimulating the neighborhood. The negative-connectivity target–control contrast survives sensory-rating adjustment and resembles a previously published depression circuit. But site, connectivity, and field geometry remain bundled; BOLD decrease is not a direct assay of neuronal inhibition; no patient outcomes or repeated-treatment effects were measured.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

- **Novelty:** The useful increment is the individualized target-versus-nearby-active-control test with concurrent downstream readout. It is not the first demonstration that TMS has remote effects or that connectivity can guide targeting. The authors' stronger priority claim was not independently established here.
- **Baselines:** Add a nearby active placement with documented target sparing, sensory matching, and field maps—not only sham or a generic scalp coordinate. Nonsignificant sensory-rating differences are not formal equivalence tests.
- **Sign and dose:** Negative targets received mean modeled on-target fields of 79.83 V/m versus 68.34 V/m for positive targets; positive targets also differed less from their controls in connectivity. Equalize effective dose and connectivity separation before treating the interaction as a clean FC-sign experiment.
- **Model validation:** Test a surrogate's predicted target–control response difference on held-out perturbation trials, then stratify by pre-pulse state. The new paper did not test state-triggered stimulation, dynamical controllability, or a learned controller.
- **Translation:** Keep three endpoints separate: predicted response, measured circuit engagement, and durable clinical benefit. Neither spatial resemblance to a depression circuit nor a separate clinical trial closes the mediation chain between them.

## One-paragraph takeaway

A personalized coordinate is not yet a personalized intervention. First show that engaging it changes the intended circuit more than a nearby, perceptually comparable alternative; then ask whether that difference is predictable across states and useful for patients. Today's new preprint earns the first step more convincingly than a condition-versus-rest map, while leaving dose matching, state dependence, and therapeutic relevance open. Pair its empirical assay with computational predictions and a clinical objective, but do not pretend the three papers jointly constitute a validated control loop.
