## Theme

Learn the loop, not just the brain coordinates.

## Short overview

One new computational full-text preserve, paired with existing network-targeting and clinical combination-treatment context. The new preprint makes a useful mechanistic point: action-dependent sensory feedback and ongoing plasticity can jointly sustain attractors, even in a feedforward neural network. That is a reason to model the environment and learning history—not evidence that reward signals are unnecessary in real brains or that an attractor has become a treatment target.

## Ranked list

1. **Adaptive behaviors emerge from Hebbian-like plasticity through flexible attractors**
   Adjacent inspiration for intervention design; directly relevant computational neuroscience and modeling. New full-text preserve; bioRxiv v1 posted November 19, 2026, not peer reviewed.
   Why it matters: Six local plasticity rules can seek an action-contingent sensory signal without an explicit reward objective. Prospective configuration captures the tested behavioral signatures across the reported settings, but task-specific parameter selection and simplified feedback matter. The strongest transferable idea is that the relevant state includes activity, plastic weights, and environmental contingencies.

2. **Heterogeneous Negative Gray Matter Volume Deviations Converge on a Shared Depression Circuit Associated With Symptoms and rTMS Response**
   Directly relevant existing network neuroscience and translational targeting context; preserved November 18. Repository note consulted, not a new primary-source audit.
   Why it matters: Large imaging replication supports a convergent circuit description, while the treatment association comes from only 25 retrospective, open-label rTMS cases. A reproducible map is not a validated control model, and reference-connectome anticorrelation is not inhibition.

3. **A randomized double-blind sham-controlled preliminary study of a 5-week protocol of repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation (rTMS) combined with cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) for gambling disorder in Indonesia**
   Directly relevant existing clinical neuromodulation/psychotherapy context; preserved September 24. Repository note consulted, not newly audited today.
   Why it matters: Among 27 adults receiving CBT, active rTMS did not outperform sham on the primary gambling-severity trajectory. Faster secondary craving/cognition changes remain process hypotheses. Improvement within a combined-treatment package cannot establish synergy or the optimal timing of its components.

## Most relevant paper

**Adaptive behaviors emerge from Hebbian-like plasticity through flexible attractors**

Li and colleagues compare local learning rules in small simulated networks whose choices determine their next sensory input. With prospective configuration, ongoing activity and weight changes produce interpretable activity–weight attractors; changing feedback timing can change persistence statistics. This is stronger than labeling an observed embedding an attractor, because the model's equations and environment generate the behavior. It remains a sufficiency argument in toy systems, not identification of the learning rule used by animals or a demonstration of therapeutic controllability.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

- **State definition:** Include plasticity and action-dependent input in the plant. A fixed-weight neural state model can miss memory stored in weights and persistence maintained through environmental feedback.
- **Mechanistic baseline:** Compare neural recurrence with world-in-the-loop recurrence. Yoked or replayed sensory input, altered action–input contingencies, and frozen plasticity are important proposed tests; not all were established experimentally here.
- **Fair comparison:** “No reward objective” describes local updates, not an untuned research pipeline. The study selects operating points using task performance, and some analyses select different settings. A locked-parameter, held-out-task comparison against reward-learning baselines remains necessary.
- **Distributional restraint:** A power-law-versus-exponential comparison is not a complete test against lognormal, truncated-power-law, or heterogeneous-mixture alternatives. HMM states summarize simulated choices; they do not identify discrete biological brain states.
- **Clinical bridge:** The existing depression circuit note provides targeting hypotheses, not causal dynamics. The CBT/rTMS trial provides an active-versus-sham augmentation test, not proof that combining two plausible mechanisms improves the primary outcome. Neither validates the preprint in patients.

## One-paragraph takeaway

A brain-state coordinate, a learning mechanism, and a useful intervention are different achievements. Today's new preserve earns a mechanistic modeling claim by specifying how plasticity and contingent input create persistent behavior; it does not earn a clinical control claim. The practical next experiment is to manipulate the feedback loop while holding sensory exposure and treatment dose comparable, then test whether the predicted change in persistence occurs. For treatment stacks, timing and contingency should be explicit experimental variables rather than decoration around a target map.

Abstract-only discovery screen, not a preserved note. Thirty patients were randomized between partly remote and entirely in-person hypnosis. Reported completion supports feasibility; the pain-change contrast has a 95% CI spanning zero. This is neither a hypnosis-versus-control efficacy test nor established equivalence of delivery modes.
- **CBT plus interventional psychiatry:** [Accelerated transcranial magnetic stimulation integrated with intensive exposure and response prevention therapy for refractory obsessive-compulsive disorder](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpsychires.2026.11.037), November 13. Abstract-only discovery screen, not a preserved note. Five patients received a five-day combined aTMS/iERP course with reported tolerability and improvement. This uncontrolled feasibility series does not identify either component's contribution, an interaction, or an optimal sequence. ERP is the relevant behavioral treatment component; the existing randomized CBT/rTMS note above remains the stronger augmentation-design context.
