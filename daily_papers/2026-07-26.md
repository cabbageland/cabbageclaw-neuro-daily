## Short overview

The top fresh preserve today is **Proximity to an SGC-DLPFC Individualized Functional Target and outcomes in large rTMS clinical trials for Treatment-Resistant Depression**. It matters because current precision-neuromodulation rhetoric keeps sneaking in a double credit. First it says individualized functional targets are mechanistically superior. Then it points to positive clinical outcomes achieved with good protocols and good neuronavigation as if those outcomes automatically prove the individualized-target idea. This paper forces the issue harder than most of the literature does. Across 501 patients pooled from the THREE-D and CARTBIND trials, retrospective individualized SGC and causal-depression-circuit targets mostly sat within the stimulated neighborhood of the actual group target, and being closer to them did not predict HRSD change, response, remission, or target-connectivity effects.

That does not prove personalization is fake. The paper is constrained by 10-minute single-echo resting-state scans, relatively large voxels, and cohort and scanner variation that could easily blunt individualized target estimation. But it does mean the field cannot keep pretending that any positive TMS outcome achieved near a connectivity-informed coordinate automatically validates individualized targeting. If a strong neuronavigated group target already lands within the effective electric-field footprint of most individualized targets, then personalized-connectivity claims need a better causal demonstration than retrospective vibes.

The best existing calibration anchor remains **Efficacy of Personalized Connectivity-Guided Accelerated Brain Stimulation in a Naturalistic Treatment-Resistant Depression Population**. That older note still matters because it shows the opposite side of the same story: a small naturalistic positive paper with plausible personalization logic and much weaker evidence. Read together, the older TAO-TMS note and the new 501-patient null do something useful. They separate “interesting targeting idea” from “clinically demonstrated targeting gain.”

The best existing computational and perturbation anchor remains **A Whole-Brain Dynamical Framework Linking Resting-State Activity to TMS-Evoked Responses**. No fresh computational or neuroengineering paper I checked today beat it on intervention-legible modeling. The newest nearby hardware paper, **Neuromorphic Silicon Neuron Controller for Adaptive Deep Brain Stimulation in Parkinson's Disease**, looks like real low-power controller engineering, but still reads more like closed-loop device architecture than like a paper that changes how we should reason about psychiatric targeting, biomarker design, or patient stratification.

## Most relevant paper

**Proximity to an SGC-DLPFC Individualized Functional Target and outcomes in large rTMS clinical trials for Treatment-Resistant Depression**

This gets the top rank because it does something rare and genuinely healthy for the field: it takes a beloved precision-psychiatry idea and asks it to survive contact with a large dataset where the comparator is already competent. The authors retrospectively compute individualized targets from baseline resting-state fMRI using both classic sgACC anticorrelation logic and the broader causal depression circuit, then ask whether patients do better when the actual neuronavigated target used in treatment landed closer to those individualized targets. Across 501 patients, the answer is basically no.

The most useful detail is not merely the null p values. It is the geometry. Median distance from the stimulated group target to the individualized target was only about 12.8 millimeters for the SGC version and 13.8 millimeters for the CDC version, with most individualized targets already within 20 millimeters. Given a TMS electric field that spans a few centimeters and a less focal MagVenture B70 coil, the field may already be stimulating enough of the same cortical neighborhood that simple coordinate personalization buys less than the rhetoric suggests. That is a much more serious challenge to the iFT story than another small underpowered positive paper would have been.

The paper also usefully exposes how entangled the current precision story still is. The null could mean that individualized targeting really adds little on top of a strong group target. But it could also mean that the imaging quality was too legacy to estimate reliable patient-level targets in the first place. The scans were single-echo, only 10 minutes long, and used relatively large voxels. The authors are appropriately cautious about this, and that caution matters. A negative result on noisy target estimation is not the same thing as a negative result on the entire targeting hypothesis.

Still, the field should not get to hide behind that caveat forever. If individualized targeting only works when scan quality is better than what clinical trials actually obtain, then it is not yet a practical precision method. That is why this paper matters. It forces future personalization claims to prove added value over a well-placed group target rather than over weaker heuristics.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

- **For precision-TMS rhetoric:** a positive outcome near a connectivity-informed target no longer counts as evidence that individualized targeting itself was the causal ingredient.
- **For biomarker logic:** SGC-based and CDC-based individualized targets look less distinct in practice than their branding suggests, since their functional maps and target coordinates largely collapse toward the same neighborhood here.
- **For clinical trial design:** the real test is no longer individualized targeting versus BeamF3-style heuristics. The real test is individualized targeting versus a strong neuronavigated group target plus modern protocol optimization.
- **For computational and perturbation work:** fresh controller or neuromorphic papers still need to translate into intervention-legible targeting or state-estimation logic before they deserve the same attention as clinically grounded circuit papers.
- **For CBT-plus-interventional combinations:** the fresh July pass did not beat the current meta-analytic anchor, which still contributes more useful implementation logic than scattered adjunctive reports.
- **For hypnosis and hypnotherapy:** today's surfaced hypnosis papers leaned toward procedural pain-control applications rather than mechanism-rich psychiatric or state-representation work, so the existing hypnosis-consciousness note remains the better keep.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The useful July 26 lesson is that precision psychiatry does not get free credit for being clever. If an individualized target usually lands inside the same effective stimulation neighborhood as a strong group target, then the field has to prove what extra value personalization really adds and under what imaging conditions it can add it reliably. Until then, a big clean null like this is more useful than another triumphant small study, because it tells us exactly where the burden of proof should move next.
