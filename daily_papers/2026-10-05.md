## Short overview

The new preserve is **Personalized Network-Guided Neuromodulation Enhances Human Working Memory**.

It is worth keeping because it does the thing many precision-stimulation papers only gesture at. The authors use resting and task fMRI to define participant-specific working-memory networks, select individualized TMS targets, run simultaneous TMS-fMRI across 5, 10, and 20 Hz stimulation, classify each person's optimal and suboptimal frequency, and then test those settings across a repeated-session crossover intervention.

The result is useful because it refuses the universal-frequency story. Optimal stimulation improved delayed matching-to-sample reaction time at working-memory delays without a processing-speed control effect, and frequency by itself did not explain the effect. The caveat is equally important: this is N = 19 healthy adults, no sham neuromodulation arm, and the real-time decoder needed a behavioral fallback in 9 of 19 participants. That makes it a good proof of concept, not a therapeutic victory lap.

## Most relevant paper

**Personalized Network-Guided Neuromodulation Enhances Human Working Memory**

This is the most relevant paper today because it gives personalization enough machinery to be falsifiable. The authors first train a working-memory fMRI decoder on an independent 90-participant dataset. For the intervention cohort, they derive personalized functional networks from resting and N-back fMRI, identify the network components most relevant to working-memory decoding, and pick individualized cortical TMS targets from subject-specific connectivity maps constrained by TMS accessibility and group-level high-connectivity priors.

Then they test frequency instead of assuming it. In a pre-neuromodulation TMS-fMRI session, each participant receives 5, 10, and 20 Hz stimulation interleaved with N-back blocks. Decoder output, backed by a predefined behavioral fallback when the decoder cannot decide, is used to designate optimal and suboptimal frequencies. In the crossover phase, participants receive three consecutive optimal-frequency sessions and three consecutive suboptimal-frequency sessions, separated by washout, with working-memory and processing-speed tasks after stimulation.

The behavioral result is promising but should stay disciplined. On delayed matching to sample, optimal stimulation produced faster responses by Day 3 at 4 s and 12 s delays, while suboptimal stimulation did not. The 4 s Day 3 optimal condition was also faster and slightly more accurate than suboptimal. The processing-speed control task did not show the same stimulation-specific effect. But the sample is small, healthy, and below planned enrollment; the decoder failed as the sole real-time chooser in 47 percent of participants; and the active comparator is suboptimal frequency, not sham.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

- **For personalized TMS:** the paper is valuable because it personalizes more than one layer. Target, frequency, and state readout are all explicit variables.
- **For stimulation frequency:** the result argues against universal "best frequency" thinking. The tested frequency only matters through its interaction with the individual target and network state.
- **For adaptive neuromodulation:** today's paper is less mature than a deployed closed-loop controller, but it is a serious scaffold: rich sensing first, parameter selection second, repeated-session behavioral test third.
- **For network neuroscience:** personalized functional networks are not used as decorative biomarkers. They define both the readout space and the target-selection logic.
- **For clinical translation:** do not inflate this into treatment evidence. Healthy working-memory enhancement is not depression, psychosis, ADHD, aging, or cognitive impairment.
- **For methods:** the most honest feature is the reported decoder brittleness. A system that needs behavioral fallback in nearly half the sample is still a proof of concept, not an autonomous controller.
- **For CBT plus interventional psychiatry:** fresh visible items included tDCS plus CBT for chronic pain, TMS plus CBT-I for mild cognitive impairment, and the existing NIBS-plus-psychotherapy meta-analysis anchor. None displaced today's state-and-parameter neuromodulation paper.
- **For hypnosis and hypnotherapy:** recent hypnosis/hypnotherapy items surfaced, including hypnotherapy-vs-CBT depression/PTSD context and migraine/outpatient depression hypnosis papers. They remained clinical context rather than stronger state-change mechanism notes today.

## One-paragraph takeaway

October fifth is a good antidote to lazy precision-stimulation rhetoric. The preserved paper is not important because it proves TMS can enhance working memory in every useful setting. It is important because it shows what a real personalization claim has to carry: individual network maps, an online state readout, parameter testing, a comparator, and a behavior that moves in the predicted condition. The field still needs larger sham-controlled and clinical tests, but this is the right kind of proof of concept: not "we found the target," but "we measured which target-frequency-state interaction actually behaved like an intervention."
