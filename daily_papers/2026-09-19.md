## Short overview

The new preserve today is **On the need of individually optimizing temporal interference stimulation of human brains due to inter-individual variability**. It is not a shiny clinical result. Good. It is the sort of methods paper that prevents shiny clinical results from becoming nonsense.

The paper tests temporal interference stimulation across 25 individualized head models and six targets: right hippocampus, left DLPFC, left motor cortex, right amygdala, right caudate, and left thalamus. The main lesson is severe: optimal temporal-interference montages vary substantially across people, common and literature montages lose focality, and random montage perturbations can move focality by centimeters. Individually optimized two-pair TI improves focality by up to 4.4 cm compared with common or literature montages, and the sensitivity analysis shows losses up to 9.27 cm under random montage changes. A simple right-hippocampus phantom recording supports the direction of the modeling claim, though it does not substitute for in vivo intracranial validation.

For cabbageland, this matters because deep noninvasive stimulation is always tempted to sell itself as access to a named structure. The access claim is only as good as the field model. A nucleus accumbens, hippocampus, thalamus, or amygdala target is not a magic word; it is a geometry, tissue-conductivity, electrode-placement, and optimization problem.

The existing **reinforcement learning-based closed-loop EEG-TMS** note remains the best adaptive-control anchor: it learns a subject/session-specific mu-rhythm phase from MEP feedback instead of predeclaring a universal phase target. The existing **two-array TI feasibility**, **TIS for MDD**, and **subthalamic TI for Parkinson's disease** notes are the right context for today's new preserve because they show the same field moving from modeling, to psychiatric biomarker work, to small blinded clinical tests. A fresh NeuroImage hit, **Ventral striatum temporal interference brain stimulation enhances the reward-positivity event-related potential and reduces anxiety**, is extremely on-lane for combining deep-target TI with emotion-regulation practice, but full text could not be accessed in this run beyond metadata and abstract routes. ## Most relevant paper

**On the need of individually optimizing temporal interference stimulation of human brains due to inter-individual variability**

This is the most relevant preserve today because it calls out a basic targeting sin. Temporal interference stimulation sounds deep and precise, but if the montage is copied from an average head, an earlier paper, or a common template, the actual field can land badly enough to make the intervention claim fragile.

The study uses individualized head models and ROAST-derived lead fields to optimize HD-TES, two-array TI, and two-pair TI across six targets. It compares individualized optimization against common MNI-derived montages, literature montages, random montage perturbations, and one agar head phantom recording setup around the right hippocampus. The useful result is not just that "individual differences matter." The useful result is that focality losses are large enough to be intervention-changing.

The caveat is that this is still mostly modeling. The phantom validation is directionally useful but preliminary, and the field still needs direct in vivo measurement during TI. So the preserve is not "TI is ready for deep psychiatric targeting." The preserve is "deep stimulation claims without individualized field optimization should be treated as under-specified."

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

- **For temporal interference stimulation:** common montages should be treated as weak baselines, not serious individualized targeting.
- **For deep noninvasive targets:** named structures are not evidence of engagement; field modeling and validation are the evidence.
- **For clinical trials:** a null TI trial without individual optimization may be a targeting failure before it is a biological failure.
- **For methods papers:** focality loss in centimeters is a useful reporting choice because it maps onto circuit anatomy.
- **For adaptive stimulation:** the spatial lesson matches the temporal lesson from closed-loop EEG-TMS: optimize against the actual person and session, not the average story.
- **For ventral striatum / nucleus accumbens TI:** reward-circuit and OCD/anxiety papers are interesting, but the targeting pipeline has to earn the anatomical claim.
- **For hypnosis/hypnotherapy:** the current biomarker lane continues to point toward state monitoring and representation change, not generic relaxation branding.
- **For CBT-plus-interventional psychiatry:** the useful combination question remains whether the intervention changes a measurable state variable that therapy can exploit, not whether two branded treatments were stacked.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The September nineteenth lesson is that noninvasive deep targeting is not granted by vocabulary. Temporal interference may become useful, but only if the field model, montage, and validation are individualized enough to make the target claim believable. For cabbageland, the steal-worthy move is to treat stimulation parameters as inference objects: spatial montages, timing phases, dose, and behavioral context should all be estimated against measured anatomy and state, then checked against target engagement.
