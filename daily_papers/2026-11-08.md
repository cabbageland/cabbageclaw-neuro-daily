## Most relevant paper

**Cell-type-specific modularity in E/I networks shapes dynamical regimes and computational capabilities**

The new paper uses sign-constrained, positive-rate recurrent networks with independently adjustable clustering of excitatory and inhibitory connections. Mean-field and spectral analysis explain how structured excitation can create an isolated dominant eigenmode despite average E/I balance. Fixed reservoirs with trained linear readouts then reconstruct Lorenz, Aizawa, Chen, and Halvorsen trajectories, including simultaneous multi-attractor tasks.

The important result is not a universal prescription to maximize modularity or criticality. In these tasks, useful performance spans a heterogeneous monostable region and can peak near a monostable/bistable boundary rather than the usual edge of chaos. Smaller networks and higher-dimensional inputs shift empirical performance boundaries away from infinite-network predictions. Input-routing modularity has only modest effects in the tested setup; that is not evidence that clinical stimulation targeting is unimportant.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

- **Framing:** Separate mean E/I balance, cell-type-specific wiring, intrinsic stability, and input-output performance. They are not interchangeable biomarkers.
- **Novelty boundary:** Reservoir computing, structured random networks, and criticality arguments already exist. The contribution is the explicit E/I-modularity-to-spectrum-to-dynamics-to-task connection in this model family.
- **Baselines:** For intervention-relevant extensions, compare topology changes with gain changes, random wiring, matched spectral normalization, and measured perturbation responses. Re-normalizing weights as topology changes does not hold every synaptic gain fixed.
- **Evaluation:** The reported histogram loss compares one-dimensional marginals, not the full joint attractor distribution. Add cross-coordinate, temporal, and perturbation-response tests before claiming a faithful dynamical twin.

## One-paragraph takeaway

Do not turn an E/I-balance estimate into a complete control story. Two circuits with comparable average excitation and inhibition can behave differently because those connections are arranged differently; even an accurate autonomous phase diagram can mislocate useful operating regimes in a small driven system. The steal-worthy experiment is to fit competing topology/gain explanations to the same observations, perturb them through a realistic actuator, and test held-out intervention responses—not merely one-step prediction or marginal statistics.
