## Theme

A reproducible disease map is not yet a validated stimulation target.

## Short overview

Today's new preserve connects heterogeneous negative gray-matter deviations in depression to a shared functional circuit. Its network replication is substantial; its rTMS evidence is a retrospective association in 25 open-label cases, not a targeting trial. Two existing notes supply the missing engineering questions: what tissue/network does a coil actually engage, and what input map would make a state controllable? One new full-text note; two explicitly reused context notes.

## Ranked list

1. **Heterogeneous Negative Gray Matter Volume Deviations Converge on a Shared Depression Circuit Associated With Symptoms and rTMS Response**
   Directly relevant clinical/translational neuromodulation and network neuroscience; new full-text preserve. Advanced Science, November 15, 2026.
   Why it matters: Discovery and replication cohorts contain 105 and 1,265 patients, respectively, but only 73 and 600 have qualifying negative-deviation clusters. Circuit topography replicates across cohorts (spatial r = 0.72). Target connectivity correlates with rTMS improvement in a separate n = 25 sample (r = -0.41), without demonstrating prospective benefit or patient-specific structural targeting.

2. **Precision Network Modeling of Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation Across Individuals Suggests Therapeutic Targets and Potential for Improvement**
   Directly relevant existing methods/neuroengineering and personalized-targeting context; preserved March 26. Repository note consulted, not a new primary-source audit.
   Why it matters: A group-level coordinate is not a patient-specific stimulation field. Individual network mapping plus electric-field modeling asks which networks a candidate coil placement actually engages; modeled selectivity is still not clinical superiority.

3. **Feasible closed-loop stimulation for neural latent dynamics control**
   Directly relevant existing computational neuroscience/control and interface-design context; November 7 bioRxiv preprint, preserved November 10. Repository note consulted, not a new primary-source audit.
   Why it matters: Sensing, actuator geometry, and identified input dynamics jointly limit feedback control. A reproducible normative-connectivity map supplies none of those causal input dynamics by itself; the control paper tests them in simulation, not psychiatric patients.

## Most relevant paper

**Heterogeneous Negative Gray Matter Volume Deviations Converge on a Shared Depression Circuit Associated With Symptoms and rTMS Response**

Han and colleagues fit age/sex normative gray-matter models, seed each patient's extreme negative deviations into a reference functional connectome, and identify convergence beyond anatomically constrained randomized seeds. The resulting circuit is reproducible across datasets and connectomes. However, "atrophy" means a cross-sectional statistical deviation, not observed tissue loss, and "network damage" means normative coupling, not measured physical damage. The treatment sample contributes previously published stimulation coordinates and outcomes, not individual structural MRI. The proposed DLPFC peak therefore remains a group-derived candidate, not a validated personalized prescription.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

- **Novelty:** Derive a candidate circuit from heterogeneous structural deviations rather than start with an a priori sgACC seed. This complements established connectivity-guided targeting; spatial agreement with known targets is not incremental efficacy.
- **Baseline:** Prospectively compare standard targeting, sgACC connectivity, individual network/E-field targeting, and the new circuit rule with matched dose and independent outcomes. Do not compare correlations from different cohorts as though they were a head-to-head trial.
- **Network validity:** Keep the reference connectome fixed while randomizing seed locations with geometry and cortical/subcortical assignment preserved. This tests above-null convergence, not uniqueness to depression or causal spread.
- **Fragility:** Connectivity/outcome association becomes nonsignificant after adjustment for available treatment characteristics (permutation p = 0.065); distance/outcome association survives that separate adjustment (p = 0.024). Stronger deviation thresholding removes significant clusters with the discovery connectome, though not the larger independent connectome.
- **Target stability:** The strongest-negative DLPFC coordinate changes across reference connectomes. Broad circuit reproducibility and precise target-coordinate reproducibility are different endpoints.
- **Control:** A static connectivity score is neither a measured brain state nor a stimulation-to-state transfer function. No closed loop or patient-specific causal control model is established here.

## One-paragraph takeaway

Build three separate audits before calling a target precise: disease-map replication, individualized physical engagement, and prospective intervention benefit. Today's paper meaningfully advances the first and supplies a small retrospective hint for the third; the two context notes make the second and the missing control logic explicit. The steal-worthy experiment freezes the normative model and target-selection rule, measures individual E-fields and target engagement, and randomizes against established targeting with equal treatment exposure. Otherwise a stable group map can become a very elaborate way to rediscover a familiar coordinate.
