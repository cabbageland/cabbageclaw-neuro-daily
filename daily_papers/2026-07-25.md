## Short overview

The top fresh preserve today is **Convergent Network Localization of Brain Stimulation Targets for Trait Anxiety**. It gets the top rank because it does something much rarer than another connectome-colored targeting claim. It makes several ugly natural experiments answer the same question and then checks whether they converge. Lesions that cause anxiety, left-prefrontal TMS sites that relieve or worsen anxiety, individualized TMS-site connectivity in a 300-person depression cohort, and subthalamic DBS sites in Parkinson's disease all point toward a common trait-anxiety circuit. ## Most relevant paper

**Convergent Network Localization of Brain Stimulation Targets for Trait Anxiety**

This gets the top rank because it stops target discovery from pretending that one attractive dataset is enough. Anxiety targeting has been stuck between generic right-prefrontal habits, correlative imaging, and symptom spillover from depression trials. This paper forces a harder standard. It asks whether lesions that cause anxiety, TMS sites that change anxiety, individualized connectivity from a fixed TMS coordinate, and DBS sites far away in the subthalamic nucleus all implicate the same circuit once depression is controlled.

The answer is usefully yes. Across seven datasets totaling 936 people, the lesion and TMS maps converge, the combined TMS and lesion maps correlate at r equals 0.68, and the individualized-connectivity TMS dataset reproduces a similar circuit while also predicting anxiety change. Then the paper does the part that makes it more than a connectome mood board: STN DBS overlap with the convergent anxiety circuit predicts anxiety worsening even though the circuit was not built from subthalamic targets. That is exactly the kind of cross-modal validation target papers usually avoid.

The trait-versus-state distinction also matters. The circuit is specific to trait anxiety rather than transient state anxiety, which makes the target much more interesting for chronic psychiatric intervention logic and much less compatible with a lazy "all anxiety is just arousal" story. The proposed peak in the right superior frontal gyrus, extending through medial prefrontal and posterior dorsolateral territory, gives a concrete place for future prospective trials to aim.

The most practically uncomfortable result is also one of the most useful. In the fixed-coordinate TMS cohort, sites more negatively connected to the anxiety circuit were associated with anxiety worsening. That suggests some depression-optimized targets may be anti-helpful for anxious symptom burden. If that holds up prospectively, it would explain a lot of mushy clinical outcomes and force more honest symptom-level target assignment.

The caveats are real. This is still a natural-experiment synthesis rather than a direct randomized anxiety-target trial. Imaging and localization quality vary across datasets. And in this run, the publisher full text for the July 2026 American Journal of Psychiatry version was bot-walled, so the detailed inspection relied on the full PMC-hosted preprint that explicitly notes the peer-reviewed update is now published. But the paper still clears the preserve bar comfortably because the underlying circuit logic is stronger than the usual branding-heavy precision-psychiatry fare.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

- **For anxiety target discovery:** a target should now be expected to survive convergence across lesions, TMS, individualized connectivity, and DBS rather than only one modality or one symptom scale.
- **For symptom-specific neuromodulation:** trait anxiety looks targetable as its own circuit object, not just as depression's noisy sidecar.
- **For safety interpretation:** anxiety worsening after TMS should be treated as target information, not only as generic adverse-event clutter.
- **For personalization claims:** individualized connectivity adds real signal here, but only modestly, which is a better argument for improving imaging quality than for declaring victory.
- **For computational control papers:** the fresh dynamic-neural-manifolds-on-neuromorphic-hardware paper is smart and worth watching, but it is still controller architecture more than intervention-legible neuroscience.
- **For standing-interest CBT combinations:** the new panic-disorder rTMS-plus-CBT trial and the coaching-plus-rTMS pilot are useful calibration, but they still do not beat the current archive anchor on design logic across studies.
- **For standing-interest hypnosis and hypnotherapy:** the latest physiology and follow-up trials are real, but they still do not beat the current hypnosis-consciousness note on mechanistic clarity.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The useful July twenty-fifth lesson is that brain-stimulation targets should not be allowed to hide behind one pretty map or one convenient trial. The best fresh paper today earns attention because it makes trait-anxiety targeting answer to converging causal evidence and then says something clinically inconvenient but probably true: circuits that help depression-like symptoms may not be the circuits that help anxiety, and some may even worsen it. That is a better research standard than another round of precision-neuromodulation branding, and it should make future target papers work much harder.
