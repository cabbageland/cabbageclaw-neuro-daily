## Most relevant paper

**Microscopic Neuromodulation with Locally Enhanced Electric Field**

Genelioglu and colleagues combine finite-element field modeling, MEMS fabrication, and acute two-photon imaging. An aluminum tip insulated with alumina and Parylene C is driven by voltage pulses; the proposed mechanism is localized capacitive/displacement-current stimulation rather than an exposed electrode or a microcoil. Fabricated 3 versus 5 micrometer tips and 0.5 versus 1 micrometer Parylene coatings produce calcium-response differences consistent with the design predictions. That is a useful prototype result, not a complete biophysical identification. The reported DC leakage measurement has a 1 mA detection limit, too coarse to establish negligible leakage at neural-stimulation scales; a 100–150 micrometer response maximum also cannot simply be equated with the simulated near-tip field decay.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

- **Novelty:** Preserve the sharp, fully insulated tip and its fabrication/design argument. Capacitive stimulation and field enhancement are not new physical phenomena; an empirical comparison with conventional capacitive electrodes is still needed.
- **Mechanism baseline:** Measure leakage with appropriate sensitivity under the actual pulse waveforms, characterize impedance and dielectric integrity, and compare sharp/blunt insulated tips, exposed electrodes, and no-output trials. Thin insulation changes both capacitive coupling and vulnerability to defects.
- **Recruitment baseline:** Match measured tissue fields or delivered exposure, not just generator voltage. Record spikes alongside calcium, test neuron/probe orientation directly, and distinguish direct activation from recurrent network recruitment and acute insertion effects.
- **Statistical baseline:** Animals, cells, and repeated trials are not interchangeable independent samples. The N = 3 feasibility experiment and t-tests do not establish robust between-animal effects; a nested analysis with calibrated doses is the next step.
- **Control/framing:** Geometry and waveform belong inside the actuator map. The TMS coupling context shows the next modeling layer; the depression-circuit context shows why even a sound actuator map is not a therapeutic targeting rule.
- **Safety/transfer:** Orientation selectivity is a modeling prediction; chronic safety, multichannel crosstalk, and clinical usefulness remain untested. No closed-loop controller, learned biomarker, or patient-specific treatment policy is demonstrated.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The steal-worthy move is to test the entire chain without letting success at one layer certify the next: geometry and coating determine the field; fields recruit particular neuronal elements; recruitment changes network state; a network perturbation may or may not improve symptoms. Today's prototype advances the first link and supplies preliminary calcium evidence for the second. The clean next experiment combines sensitive leakage measurements, calibrated waveforms, simultaneous electrophysiology and imaging, and animal-level replication before anyone sells thin insulation as chronic safety or local fluorescence as precise circuit control.
