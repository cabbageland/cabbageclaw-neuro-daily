# 2026-11-22

## Theme

Equal pulse counts are not yet equal brain-state interventions.

## Short overview

One new full-text preserve, with two existing notes supplying network/modeling and perturbation-method context. A within-person comparison of five daily versus five same-day parietal cTBS sessions finds no detectable difference in delayed bilateral parietal connectivity change. Useful protocol-design evidence, but a fixed treatment order, broad uncertainty, and no formal equivalence test prevent the stronger claim that schedule does not matter. The accompanying notes explain why a connectivity endpoint, a dynamical representation, and an experimentally measured input map are different objects.

## Ranked list

1. **Accelerated vs. Daily TMS: Resting-State Functional Connectivity in a Within-Subjects Crossover Trial**
   Directly relevant clinical/translational neuromodulation and network-targeting work. New full-text preserve; medRxiv v1 posted November 20, 2026, not peer reviewed.
   Why it matters: Among 34 analyzed participants, the schedule contrast was small but imprecise (reported Cohen's d = 0.12, 95% CI [-0.45, 0.69]). All received daily treatment first. This is a useful within-person neural-endpoint comparison, not proof of neural equivalence, clinical noninferiority, or a universal cumulative-dose rule.

   Why it matters: Shared connectivity coordinates can describe modality-dependent trajectories, but descriptive mixture regions and a fitted autoregression do not establish biological attractors or a stimulation policy. A state representation must earn its predictive and causal role separately.

   Directly relevant network perturbation and neuroengineering methods. Existing preserve from November 15; repository note consulted, not newly audited today.

This supplies the right default baseline for multisite control: demonstrate what the additive input map misses before calling a larger response synergy.

## Most relevant paper

**Accelerated vs. Daily TMS: Resting-State Functional Connectivity in a Within-Subjects Crossover Trial**

Connolly and colleagues hold session count and nominal pulse dose constant while changing delivery schedule within returning participants. Individualized left-parietal DMN targets receive five 600-pulse cTBS sessions either daily or in one day, separated by a long interval between courses. The important boundary is temporal: post-treatment fMRI occurs roughly four to five days later. Similar delayed pairwise connectivity does not imply similar acute plasticity, network trajectories, or symptom benefit. Read the null contrast as uncertainty to resolve, not permission to discard scheduling from a control model.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

- **Framing:** Separate three estimands: delayed neural-endpoint difference, clinical noninferiority, and equivalence of underlying dynamics. This study addresses only the first, without a prespecified equivalence margin.
- **Causal design:** A year-long average interval may reduce stimulation carryover, but it cannot remove fixed-order confounding by time, illness course, medication, or repeat participation. Randomize/counterbalance order and include an appropriate sham comparison.
- **State estimation:** Baseline connectivity predicting its own change is not automatically a response biomarker; shared measurement noise and regression to the mean require explicit checks.
- **Control baseline:** Pair shared-state representations with measured input responses. Neither the sensory-landscape representation nor the multisite additive map alone identifies a therapeutic controller. Test a locked model prospectively across people, sessions, and devices before claiming transfer.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The steal-worthy idea is to compare schedules within people while measuring a predefined network endpoint. The necessary correction is to preserve uncertainty: five matched cTBS sessions and one delayed connectivity measure cannot establish that timing is irrelevant. For future intervention work, estimate both the evolving state and the effect of inputs, retain additive and baseline-only comparators, and ask whether timing changes the trajectory even when the eventual scalar endpoint looks similar.
