# 2026-11-24

## Theme

A sequential trigger is not yet a causal sensor—or a therapeutic controller.

## Short overview

One new full-text neuroengineering preserve, with existing computational/network-modeling and clinical neuromodulation notes supplying complementary tests. An ECG-derived sympathetic-activity pipeline makes its calibration, burst threshold, persistence rule, and detection denominators unusually explicit. Its important boundary is equally explicit: replay decisions use past envelope samples, but the envelope was preprocessed offline and calibration was aligned retrospectively to a known event. The transferable contribution is a reproducible sensing evaluation, not a demonstrated real-time closed loop or treatment effect.

## Ranked list

1. **A reproducible event-driven analytical framework for ECG-derived skin sympathetic nerve activity analysis: towards afferent signal processing for closed-loop neuromodulation**
   Directly relevant methods/neuroengineering; adjacent to psychiatric intervention design. New full-text preserve; Frontiers in Bioengineering and Biotechnology, published November 4, 2026.
   Why it matters: A personalized threshold and persistence-gated replay trigger are tested in 50 anesthetized pigs. New event-associated activation occurs in 42/49 eligible recordings, with median latency 24.0 seconds among detections. Noncausal preprocessing, retrospective calibration, surrogate physiology, and absent specificity estimates prevent calling this a deployable controller.

2. **Beyond the attractor: closed-loop stimulation design for learning multistable neural dynamics**
   Directly relevant computational neuroscience and circuit/system identification; adjacent inspiration for therapy. Existing preserve from November 7, based on bioRxiv version 1; repository note consulted, not a new full-text audit.
   Why it matters: PALDI chooses perturbations to identify partially observed circuit dynamics and separately tests downstream control. It supplies the identification/controller layer absent from the new sensing paper. All evidence is simulated, and the observation map is assumed known or calibrated separately; an imperfect physiological surrogate cannot simply be substituted without reevaluation.

3. **Transcutaneous vagus nerve stimulation enhances reward-effort efficiency in major depressive disorder**
   Directly relevant clinical/translational neuromodulation. Existing medRxiv preserve surfaced May 24; repository note consulted, not newly reread primary evidence or an updated publication-status check.
   Why it matters: The preserved report describes an acute, randomized single-blind crossover comparison in which symptom severity moderates reward-effort efficiency, with less unnecessary effort rather than indiscriminate invigoration. This supplies a behavioral objective worth separating from an autonomic signal. It does not validate SKNA as a depression biomarker, establish a neural mediator, or demonstrate durable antidepressant benefit.

## Most relevant paper

**A reproducible event-driven analytical framework for ECG-derived skin sympathetic nerve activity analysis: towards afferent signal processing for closed-loop neuromodulation**

Rahmani and colleagues extract a high-frequency ECG envelope, fuse available channels by their median, fit a baseline-informed threshold, and require sustained burst occupancy before issuing a replay trigger. Trigger presence during an imposed respiratory event is 43/50; newly generated activation is 42/49 among recordings OFF at event onset. Those are different denominators. Seven recordings have some pre-event trigger activity, but this is not a measured false-positive rate: respiratory-event timing is not ground truth for the presence or absence of sympathetic discharge. The authors explicitly acknowledge that neither preprocessing nor baseline selection is fully causal. Preserve the evaluation contract, not a claim that the loop has already been closed.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

- **Sensing baseline:** Audit the complete raw-signal-to-action path. Forward-backward filtering and event-aligned calibration remain retrospective even when downstream windows look only backward. Replace them with prospective calibration and causal filtering, then remeasure detection and latency.
- **Network/modeling baseline:** The existing PALDI note separates readout assumptions, hidden-state estimation, dynamics learning, and intervention evaluation. A sympathetic envelope is not an identified brain-network state. Before combining these ideas, test whether the observation actually distinguishes the states and input responses the controller needs.
- **Clinical objective:** More sympathetic activity, more physical effort, and better reward-effort allocation are not interchangeable endpoints. A treatment controller needs a prespecified patient-relevant objective and evidence that acting on the readout improves it.
- **Feedback comparator:** Compare closed loop with resource-matched fixed stimulation, time-shuffled/yoked triggers, and a no-intervention condition. Sensing feasibility alone cannot isolate feedback value from stimulation dose or event timing.
- **Version watch:** The bioRxiv API now lists PALDI version 2, posted November 23, under **Information-Guided Planning for Online System Identification of Partially Observed Nonlinear Dynamical Systems** (same DOI stem: [10.64898/2026.10.05.756678](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.05.756678)). The existing canonical note remains a version-1 read. No revised numerical result or claim of a substantive version change is inferred from metadata alone.
- **Next decisive experiment:** Stream raw ECG through a causal implementation, calibrate without future event annotations, independently label sympathetic activity, quantify false alarms per hour and missed-event latency, then randomize whether matched-budget stimulation follows the detected state.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The reusable lesson is to keep observation, state estimation, dynamics identification, action selection, and patient benefit separate. Today's new paper earns a useful offline sensing/triggering reference and reports the boundaries honestly. Existing circuit-identification work shows what a controller would additionally need to learn; the clinical effort-allocation note shows why the eventual objective must be more specific than changing an arousal proxy. A clean replay plot is an intermediate test, not the final experiment.
