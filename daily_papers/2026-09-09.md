## Short overview

The best new preserve today is **Cholinergic-dependent dopamine signals in mouse dorsomedial striatum are regulated by frontal but not sensory cortices**. It wins because it takes a familiar phrase, sensory-evoked striatal dopamine, and breaks it into the actual circuit parts. The Nature Communications full text shows that visual cues can evoke dopamine in anterior dorsomedial striatum partly through a local cholinergic interneuron mechanism, but primary sensory cortices are not the cortical inputs that meaningfully drive that gate. Frontal inputs, especially prelimbic and anterior cingulate projections, are the privileged route.

The important distinction is not simply dopamine versus acetylcholine. It is route-specific access to a local neuromodulatory gate. Visual and auditory cortices can project to striatum and sensory cues can reach striatal neurons, but that does not mean those projections can recruit cholinergic interneurons strongly enough to drive cholinergic-dependent dopamine release. In the full text, systemic mecamylamine reduced visual-evoked striatal dopamine to about 41% of baseline while sparing somatic dopamine-neuron calcium responses, and local mecamylamine reduced dopamine evoked by frontal corticostriatal terminal stimulation to about 38-39% across stimulation levels. Rabies tracing and slice physiology then make the anatomy-function split concrete: medial frontal inputs robustly target and drive cholinergic interneurons; primary sensory inputs mostly do not.

For cabbageland, this matters because neuromodulation too often stops at large nouns: dopamine, striatum, DLPFC, salience, reward, motivation. Today's note is a useful antidote. It says that the same broad region can receive many inputs, but only some have access to the microcircuit that changes the neuromodulatory state. That pairs naturally with the existing archive note **mPFC axons drive cognitive control enhancement during striatal stimulation**, where stimulating medial-prefrontal axons in striatum reproduced a cognitive-control benefit while stimulating local striatal neurons impaired performance. Together they argue for a sharper stimulation question: which fibers, interneurons, and local neuromodulator gates are being recruited?

The fresh clinical/translational lane had two strong signals but neither displaced the full-text circuit preserve. **An Epicenter-guided computational framework for personalized target of repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation in schizophrenia** is the most directly relevant clinical paper by topic: it proposes individualized DLPFC rTMS targets from patient-specific pathological epicenters and reports that target proximity relates to functional-connectivity normalization and PANSS negative-symptom improvement in retrospective validation. It stayed out of canonical preservation because the public routes available here exposed PubMed metadata, DOI and open-access metadata, Crossref, and Unpaywall records, but the ScienceDirect article body/PDF was blocked by 403 responses in this environment. **Neurophysiological Signatures of Negative Symptom Improvement After High-Definition Transcranial Direct Current Stimulation** remains another important schizophrenia neuromodulation signal, but it is still abstract-qualified here.

The computational/methods lane was useful. The arXiv full text **Homeostasis Revisited and Reformulated Through Hidden Markov Model Control** is worth remembering because it directly attacks a control-as-inference habit: maximizing a variational free-energy lower bound is not the same as directly maximizing the probability of desired observations under real transition dynamics. The paper's useful warning is that inference over desirable state-action paths can assume too much control over state transitions. **An Evidence-Aware Framework for EEG Microstate Analysis** is also relevant because it keeps template evidence strength and low-confidence periods instead of collapsing every scalp topography into a winner-take-all microstate label. Both are useful method signals, but today's preserve has the better full circuit-to-intervention transfer.

## Most relevant paper

**Cholinergic-dependent dopamine signals in mouse dorsomedial striatum are regulated by frontal but not sensory cortices**

This is the most relevant paper today because it turns a broad neuromodulator story into a circuit-access story. The usual sloppy version would say visual cues evoke striatal dopamine and move on. The useful version asks which cortical routes can actually recruit the local cholinergic interneuron mechanism that amplifies or shapes dopamine release.

The first result establishes the gate. Visual stimuli evoked dopamine in dorsomedial striatum, and systemic mecamylamine reduced that dopamine response to roughly 41% of baseline while sparing somatic dopamine-neuron calcium responses. The authors then show that visual stimuli recruit striatal cholinergic interneurons and acetylcholine release, so the dopamine effect is not just a midbrain firing story.

The second result makes the route-specific part hard to ignore. Primary sensory areas project to the striatum, but rabies tracing and slice physiology show that they do not strongly synapse onto dorsomedial-striatal cholinergic interneurons. Frontal areas do. Anterior cingulate and prelimbic inputs produce large excitatory currents in cholinergic interneurons, while visual and auditory inputs are weak there despite reliable input to medium spiny neurons.

The perturbation result closes the loop. Optogenetic stimulation of prelimbic/anterior cingulate terminals in dorsomedial striatum evokes acetylcholine and dopamine responses in vivo, and local mecamylamine reduces the evoked dopamine response to about 38-39% across stimulation levels. That is not a complete behavioral theory of reinforcement learning, but it is a serious causal handle.

The caveats are real. Head-fixed cue exposure is not a rich learning task. Terminal stimulation can recruit passing fibers or collaterals, including projections that also reach midbrain dopamine regions. Sensor kinetics make timing comparisons imperfect. But those limits do not erase the central useful result: striatal dopamine is partly governed by which cortical route can access the local cholinergic gate.

For intervention thinking, the lesson is clean. A stimulation field or disease lesion does not act on "the striatum" as a unit. It acts on specific afferents, local interneurons, receptors, and neuromodulator-release mechanisms. If we want psychiatric stimulation to become less decorative, this is the level of mechanism we need to keep demanding.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

- **For dopamine language:** dopamine is not just a midbrain broadcast or generic reward scalar. Local striatal circuit gates can determine when and where dopamine is released.
- **For stimulation mechanism:** the useful question is which axons and interneuron gates are recruited, not merely which region sits under the electrode, ultrasound focus, or coil field.
- **For corticostriatal psychiatry:** the paper gives a basic-science mechanism that pairs with psychiatric striatal stimulation work: frontal afferents can have privileged access to striatal state control.
- **For personalized TMS framing:** the new abstract-qualified rTMS schizophrenia paper points in the same direction at human scale: target proximity to individualized pathological epicenters may matter because different routes engage different downstream dynamics.
- **For computational control:** the HMM-control arXiv paper is a useful warning against wishful control-as-inference. The environment's transition structure matters; desirable paths are not the same as controllable paths.
- **For EEG state methods:** the evidence-aware microstate paper supports a similar anti-compression instinct: do not collapse uncertain, mixed state evidence into hard labels unless the loss of information is acceptable.
- **For standing-interest lanes:** hypnosis/hypnotherapy did not surface a stronger current-window mechanism preserve; CBT-plus-interventional psychiatry again surfaced the tDCS-plus-CBT chronic-pain RCT as useful but not central to today's circuit story.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The September ninth lesson is that the important unit is often not a region, a transmitter, or a protocol. It is a route-specific circuit gate. Today's preserve shows that salient visual stimuli can evoke striatal dopamine partly through a local cholinergic mechanism, but primary sensory inputs do not meaningfully drive that gate in anterior dorsomedial striatum; frontal inputs do. That should make us suspicious of every neuromodulation story that jumps from target name to mechanism without asking which fibers, interneurons, and local state variables were actually recruited.
