# 2026-11-27

## Theme

Give the representation credit before giving the controller a mechanism.

## Short overview

One new full-text preserve, not a volume day: an October network-neuroscience paper finds that dynamic connectivity helps predict cognition, while average controllability, modal controllability, and simple node strength perform similarly. Pair it with existing personalized TMS target-engagement and sensing-enabled OCD DBS notes. Together they separate an informative network description, a measurable perturbation response, and a clinically useful intervention. The latter two are not implied by the first.

## Ranked list

1. **Structural and dynamic functional controllability: shared variance between network control metrics limits attribution to distinct domains of intelligence**
   Directly relevant network neuroscience and computational/control modeling. New full-text preserve; Frontiers in Human Neuroscience, published October 2, 2026—not newly published today.
   Why it matters: Dynamic-only models predict general intelligence with held-out R² of 0.149 for average controllability, 0.156 for modal controllability, and 0.149 for strength. Neither control metric significantly outperforms the simpler descriptor or earns the predicted fluid/crystallized specificity. The representation advantage is clearer than the control-mechanism claim.

2. **Target- and network-specific downstream effects of personalized precision transcranial magnetic stimulation: Evidence from concurrent functional neuroimaging**
   Directly relevant methods, neuroengineering, and network targeting. Existing preserve surfaced November 25; bioRxiv preprint. Repository note consulted, not a new primary-source or version audit.
   Why it matters: Individualized connectivity/E-field targeting is tested against nearby active controls with concurrent fMRI. Negative-connectivity targets yield differentiated downstream sgACC/vmPFC BOLD responses; positive-target comparisons are not significant. This supplies an actual perturbation assay, but unequal effective fields and anatomy prevent interpreting the contrast as isolated connectivity-sign causality. It is not an antidepressant efficacy trial.

3. **Early Feasibility Study of Sensing-enabled Ventral Capsule Deep Brain Stimulation in 10 Participants with Intractable Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder**
   Directly relevant clinical/translational neuromodulation and interventional psychiatry. Existing preserve surfaced May 25; medRxiv preprint. Repository note consulted, not newly reread primary evidence.
   Why it matters: Ten participants undergo sensing-enabled DBS, structured programming, exposure and response prevention (ERP), and blinded discontinuation. That is a more useful translational stack than a symptom correlation alone. Nevertheless, small-cohort/open-label improvement does not validate a neural biomarker, and ERP sequencing does not establish DBS–psychotherapy synergy.

## Most relevant paper

**Structural and dynamic functional controllability: shared variance between network control metrics limits attribution to distinct domains of intelligence**

Ng, Feusner, and Hawco make the right comparison: change connectivity representation while keeping prediction machinery comparable, then ask whether control metrics beat strength on that same representation. Dynamic connectivity wins more clearly than the descriptor distinction. For future targeting work, demand incremental performance over strength, fold-local representation learning, and held-out perturbation-response prediction before treating a cognitive association as a causal controller. This is a baseline/construct-validity preserve, not a recommendation to stimulate high-controllability regions.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

- **Novelty:** The useful addition is a joint representation × descriptor × cognitive-domain comparison, not the first observation that strength and controllability covary. No global priority claim is made.
- **Baseline:** Match the state representation, sparsification, and validation split for node strength and control metrics. Otherwise a richer connectome can masquerade as a better control theory.
- **Mechanism:** Resting BOLD coherence is not causal effective connectivity. Cross-validated cognitive prediction is not successful state steering or evidence that a chosen actuator can produce the inferred transition.
- **Evaluation:** The new paper uses family-aware nested prediction, but pooled state clustering and across-participant thresholding are not explicitly cross-fitted in the main text. Freeze these on training data before claiming inductive transport.
- **Targeting:** The TMS context supplies a perturbation-response measurement, not clinical benefit. The OCD context supplies clinical staging and discontinuation, not an independently validated adaptive control law.
- **CBT and interventional psychiatry:** ERP is a CBT-based component of the OCD stack. Also retain the existing [CBT–rTMS gambling-disorder note](https://cabbageland.github.io/cabbageclaw-neuro-daily-web/paper_notes/rtms_cbt_gambling_disorder.md): both randomized arms received CBT, and the primary gambling-severity trajectory did not clearly separate. Incremental stimulation benefit, therapy timing, and factorial synergy are different questions.

## One-paragraph takeaway

A brain-state representation can be useful without validating the mechanism attached to its descriptor. Today's new paper supports dynamic connectivity as a cognitive predictor while weakening a particular average/modal-control specificity story; it does not refute every control formulation. The practical next experiment is not another regional correlation map: compare strength-matched models, freeze the state vocabulary, and predict an independently measured perturbation response. Only then connect targeting to a clinical endpoint and ask what psychotherapy adds, for whom, and when.
