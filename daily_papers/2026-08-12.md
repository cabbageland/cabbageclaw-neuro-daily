## Theme

## Short overview

The fresh preserve today is the August 12, 2026 *American Journal of Psychiatry* paper **Individualized Connectivity-Guided Versus Conventional Targeting of Accelerated Theta-Burst Stimulation in Depression: A Randomized, Double-Blind, Parallel-Design Trial**. It matters because it does something the precision-TMS literature often avoids: it compares multiple individualized target logics against the crude clinical default instead of merely celebrating one personalization story in isolation. Adults with major depressive disorder or bipolar II depression were randomized to accelerated iTBS targeted by the 5-centimeter rule, sgACC-negative resting-state functional connectivity, or sgACC-oriented structural connectivity from probabilistic tractography, all delivered with robotic neuronavigation across 20 sessions in 2 weeks.

The most useful surprise is that the abstract does **not** give the clean victory speech for functional connectivity targeting that the field usually wants. Structural-connectivity-guided targeting beat the 5-centimeter rule at week 2, with a least-squares mean difference of 8.79 percentage points in HAM-D reduction and Cohen's `d = 0.70`. By week 6, both structural and functional guidance beat the 5-centimeter arm, with the structural arm still numerically stronger (`d = 1.03` versus `d = 0.75`). By week 12, the between-group differences were no longer significant. That is a much more interesting result than generic "personalization works." It suggests that precision may help, but the winning ingredient may not be the field's favorite resting-state anticorrelation story alone.

There is also a real access caveat. This preserve is based on abstract-level evidence after 10 full-text acquisition attempts across publisher, DOI, PubMed, PMC, Europe PMC, Crossref, registry, and title/manuscript search routes. That lowers confidence on the exact targeting pipeline, subgroup handling, mixed-model specification, and response-remission detail. Still, head-to-head clinical comparisons between functional, structural, and folk-rule targeting are rare enough that even abstract-level signal here is strategically useful.

The fresh comparison lane was not empty. The August 11, 2026 *Acta Epileptologica* paper **Vagus nerve stimulation as an add-on therapy in patients with epilepsy: a prospective, multicenter, real-world study in China** looks solid as registry evidence, but it is mostly a cumulative-response and quality-of-life paper rather than a mechanism or control-logic paper. The August 11, 2026 *Ultrasound in Medicine and Biology* paper **Accelerating Focused Ultrasound Modeling in Heterogeneous Spinal Cord Anatomy With Vision Transformer Operator Networks** is a serious methods result with enormous speedup, but right now it is still a preclinical operator-learning acceleration story rather than a directly useful neuropsychiatric intervention note. The August 1, 2026 *Epilepsia* paper **Low-intensity focused ultrasound neuromodulation for drug-resistant epilepsy: A randomized, sham-controlled crossover trial** is valuable mainly because the randomized crossover portion was not clearly positive even though the open-label extension looked better. That makes it more useful as realism than as a preserve.

The standing-interest checks were also worth doing. In the hypnosis lane, today's PubMed refresh mostly surfaced reviews, broad integrative pieces, and a dissociative-PTSD case report, none of which beat the archive note **Decoding hypnotic consciousness: neural and experiential insights into induced and ideomotor suggestions** as a mechanism-centered anchor. In the CBT-plus-interventional lane, the fresh 2026 meta-analysis **Efficacy of repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation combined with cognitive behavioral therapy for depression** supports a small-to-moderate benefit over rTMS controls, but it is narrower and less implementation-rich than the broader archive note **Noninvasive brain stimulation combined with evidence-based psychotherapy for psychiatric disorders: A meta-analysis of optimal implementation parameters**. So today's real lesson is not "combination care wins" or "hypnosis has arrived." It is that precision-targeting claims now need to survive a more specific question: what kind of connectivity guidance actually earns its cost?

## Most relevant paper

**Individualized Connectivity-Guided Versus Conventional Targeting of Accelerated Theta-Burst Stimulation in Depression: A Randomized, Double-Blind, Parallel-Design Trial**

The strategic value here is not just that it is another depression TMS paper with the word "individualized" in the title. The useful move is that it compares three target-selection logics inside the same accelerated protocol: the old 5-centimeter rule, sgACC-negative resting-state functional connectivity, and sgACC-oriented structural connectivity. That is closer to the real precision question. If you are going to pay the imaging and workflow tax, which targeting logic actually buys anything?

The abstract says 123 participants were randomized and 119 entered the modified intention-to-treat analysis, spanning adults with major depressive disorder or bipolar II depression and at least one antidepressant failure. Everyone received 20 sessions over 2 weeks using robotic neuronavigation. The primary endpoint was percentage reduction in 17-item HAM-D at week 2, with follow-up through weeks 6 and 12. The clinically interesting pattern is asymmetrical. Structural-connectivity guidance beats the 5-centimeter rule at week 2 and remains the numerically stronger precision arm at week 6, while functional-connectivity guidance separates from the 5-centimeter rule only at week 6. That means the result is not simply "more personalization is better." It is closer to "different personalization logics buy different things on different timescales."

The caveat is that this preserve is abstract-only despite 10 full-text attempts. That blocks confidence on details that matter: exact sgACC targeting reproducibility, whether subgroup effects differ between unipolar and bipolar II depression, how robust the week-6 effect is to modeling choices, whether response/remission rates align cleanly with the continuous outcome, and how burdensome the imaging stack really was in practice. But even with those access limits, the trial is still worth preserving because it sharpens baseline choice for future precision-TMS claims. If a new targeting method cannot beat a geometric folk rule in a direct randomized comparison, the elegance story should not carry much weight.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

- **For precision-targeting claims:** this is more useful than the average personalization paper because it puts structural and functional guidance in the ring against the same conventional targeting baseline.
- **For target logic:** the abstract's most interesting message is that structural-connectivity guidance gets the cleaner early win, which complicates the field's habit of treating sgACC anticorrelation as the obvious privileged mechanism.
- **For accelerated-treatment framing:** because the protocol is matched across arms, the paper shifts the interesting question from "does accelerated iTBS work?" to "what kind of target selection changes the trajectory inside an accelerated protocol?"
- **For baseline choice:** future precision-TMS papers should have to say whether they beat serious comparators, not merely whether they can be described with prettier imaging rhetoric than the 5-centimeter rule.
- **For durability logic:** the lost significance by week 12 is a reminder that acute slope and longer-horizon maintenance remain separable design problems.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The useful August 12 lesson is that precision targeting should no longer get credit just for existing. Today's preserve matters because it compares functional-connectivity, structural-connectivity, and conventional targeting inside the same accelerated depression protocol and suggests that structural guidance may carry the sharper early antidepressant signal. The paper is access-limited here to abstract-level evidence, so confidence is lower than usual on the mechanistic and statistical details. But the result is still worth keeping because it makes the targeting question more specific and therefore more scientifically valuable.
