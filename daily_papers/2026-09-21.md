## Short overview

The two new preserves are **Ultrasound Brain-Computer Interfaces for Transcranial Closed-Loop Neuromodulation** and **Cathodal tDCS in obsessive-compulsive disorder: cognitive and neurophysiological outcomes in a double-blind sham-controlled trial**.

The ultrasound BCI paper is the architecture anchor. It is a Perspective, not a validation study, but it is valuable because it refuses sloppy device-language inflation. It defines ultrasound BCI as a system where brain activity is converted into functionally useful output, with ultrasound providing readout, state-contingent write-in, or both. More importantly, it says closed-loop acoustic neuromodulation should be judged by reproducible targeting, stable coupling, skull-adjusted exposure, safety state, controller rules, and measurable response, not by isolated imaging or open-loop stimulation demos.

The OCD tDCS paper is the clinical anchor. It randomized adults with OCD to cathodal mPFC-tDCS or sham and measured Y-BOCS, task switching, salivary BDNF, and frontal EEG. The active group improved more over time on symptoms and task-switching, but the final sample was only 15 completers, qEEG was partly missing, blinding success was not formally tested, and the BDNF story is weaker than the abstract tone because the time-by-group interaction was not significant. Keep it as a small sham-controlled clinical signal, not a settled mechanism.

For cabbageland, the shared lesson is that "closed-loop" and "mechanism" need audit trails. The ultrasound paper says what a serious state-action-response loop must record. The OCD paper shows a more clinical version of the same problem: symptoms moved, but the mechanistic readouts are not yet strong enough to explain why.

## Most relevant paper

**Ultrasound Brain-Computer Interfaces for Transcranial Closed-Loop Neuromodulation**

This is the most relevant preserve today because it gives a clean standard for the kind of intervention stack cabbageland keeps circling: sense state, estimate whether action is allowed, deliver a constrained perturbation, measure response, update cautiously, and record enough provenance that the loop can be audited. The paper is not evidence that ultrasound BCI is ready for clinic. Its value is that it says exactly what is missing before that claim would be earned.

The best phrase in the paper's logic is not a slogan but a requirement: state-action-response traceability. For every closed-loop action, the system should know the state estimate, the controller rule, the skull-adjusted acoustic field, transducer registration, coupling status, safety state, and measured response. That standard transfers directly to TMS, tDCS, DBS, RNS, psychedelics-plus-therapy timing, and any other intervention that wants to claim mechanism rather than mood lighting.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

- **For ultrasound neuromodulation:** passive imaging, open-loop tFUS, stimulation-aided BCI, and bidirectional closed-loop acoustic neuromodulation are different claims and should not be collapsed.
- **For closed-loop systems:** the right baseline is optimized open-loop stimulation, not no stimulation or an underbuilt straw control.
- **For state estimation:** EEG or fNIRS may be the fast readout while ultrasound contributes focal write-in, calibration, or localized assessment; hybrid architecture is more plausible than all-ultrasound everything.
- **For clinical tDCS:** the OCD trial supports further study of cathodal mPFC-tDCS, but it does not establish a BDNF or frontal-alpha mechanism.
- **For CBT-plus-interventional psychiatry:** the OCD tDCS paper raises a useful process-variable idea: cognitive flexibility may be the thing stimulation should improve so behavioral intervention has more traction.
- **For hypnosis/hypnotherapy:** no stronger new full-text hypnosis or hypnotherapy paper displaced today's preserves. The relevant standing frame remains state-change measurement plus response verification, not suggestion-language vibes.
- **For computational/control modeling:** temporal-basis and RL EEG-TMS notes remain the stronger implementation anchors because they turn response history into action selection rather than only describing a future closed-loop stack.

## One-paragraph takeaway

September twenty-first is a useful anti-hype day. The ultrasound BCI Perspective says that closed-loop neuromodulation becomes real only when state estimates, actions, dose, safety, and response are all traceable. The OCD tDCS trial says that small sham-controlled clinical signals are worth keeping when they include process measures, but the mechanism story still has to survive sample size, missing data, and weak biomarker interactions. The practical standard is simple: if a paper says "control," ask what state was estimated, what action was chosen, what dose hit the target, what response was measured, and what would change on the next trial.
