## Short overview

The two new preserves are **Accelerated TMS is associated with changes of subgenual cingulate-default mode network coupling in depression: an observational 7 Tesla fMRI study** and **Shallow recurrent decoders for neural and behavioural dynamics**.

The aTMS paper is the clinical anchor. It reports that a two-week left-DLPFC accelerated iTBS course in depression was associated with reduced sgACC coupling to bilateral precunei and left frontal cortex on 7-Tesla resting-state fMRI. Patients improved clinically, and sgACC-precuneus connectivity reduction correlated with mindfulness improvement, but the study is observational, unblinded, non-randomized, unshammed, small, and mostly medicated. The correct read is target-engagement clue, not causal proof.

The SHRED paper is the methods anchor. It uses a GRU plus shallow decoder to reconstruct high-dimensional neural or behavioral states from sparse time-series sensors across C. elegans, mouse, zebrafish, and human locomotion examples. The useful lesson is not that sparse decoders explain the brain; it is that state estimation can become less invasive if the sensor history is dynamically coupled to the hidden state and the model is audited against real held-out data.

For cabbageland, the shared move is simple: intervention work needs better measurement. The aTMS paper measures whether the hypothesized depression network moves after stimulation. The SHRED paper asks how much state can be inferred from limited sensors. The existing closed-loop EEG-TMS note remains the action anchor because it uses feedback to learn a stimulation state online rather than only observing it after the fact.

## Most relevant paper

**Accelerated TMS is associated with changes of subgenual cingulate-default mode network coupling in depression: an observational 7 Tesla fMRI study**

This is the most relevant preserve today because it sits exactly at the clinical mechanism boundary: left-DLPFC stimulation, sgACC-DMN coupling, depression symptoms, and a process variable related to mindfulness/emotion regulation. It does not prove that aTMS caused the network change, but it does measure the kind of network change that should be measured if the treatment rationale depends on DLPFC-sgACC-DMN modulation.

The study enrolled 22 patients with current depression and 14 healthy controls. Patients received four iTBS sessions per treatment day, five days per week, for two weeks, totaling 24,000 pulses to Beam-F3 left DLPFC. The imaging result is decreased sgACC connectivity with bilateral precunei and left frontal cortex after treatment. The clinical result is improvement in MADRS, BDI-II, mindfulness, and positive affect, with 13 responders and 7 remitters. The caveat is the whole causal story: no sham, no randomization, no blinding, small sample, concurrent clinical care, and exploratory uncorrected correlations.

So the keep is narrow but real. If future accelerated TMS trials claim network mechanism, this is the type of readout they should include, but with stronger design and individualized targeting.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

- **For accelerated TMS:** target engagement should be measured at the network level, not inferred from symptom change.
- **For depression neuromodulation:** sgACC-DMN coupling remains a useful candidate mechanism, but uncontrolled pre/post studies cannot carry causal language.
- **For personalization:** Beam-F3 is clinically practical, but it leaves open whether individualized DLPFC-sgACC targeting would produce stronger or more specific effects.
- **For sparse sensing:** SHRED is a useful reminder that temporal history can turn a few sensors into a state estimator, but reconstruction is not mechanism.
- **For closed-loop stimulation:** SHRED-style state estimation and RL EEG-TMS-style action selection are complementary pieces of the same future system.
- **For network control:** controllable directions and perturbation responses remain better intervention language than generic "connectivity changed" claims.
- **For hypnosis/hypnotherapy:** the FND/suggestion/VR computational-perspective paper was relevant to state-change framing, but accessible full text was blocked by publisher challenge routes in this run, so it stayed context rather than preserve.
- **For CBT-plus-interventional psychiatry:** no stronger new CBT-plus-neuromodulation paper beat the two preserves today; the useful combination question remains whether the intervention changes a measurable state that therapy can exploit.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The September twentieth lesson is that the intervention story is only as strong as the state measurement wrapped around it. The aTMS paper gives a plausible sgACC-DMN network readout but not causal identification. The SHRED paper gives a sparse state-estimation recipe but not causal mechanism. The direction for cabbageland is to combine these sensibly: measure the relevant state, estimate it with the least invasive reliable sensors, then use feedback to choose intervention parameters rather than pretending that a named target or decoder output is already evidence of control.
