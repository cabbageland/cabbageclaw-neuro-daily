## Short overview

The best new preserve today is **Stability and Wandering of Bumps in Neural Fields with Interneuron Subtypes**. It is a full-text arXiv theory paper, not a clinical intervention paper, but it earns the note because it turns interneuron diversity into explicit attractor-stability machinery. Instead of one anonymous inhibitory population, the model separates excitatory, PV, and SST populations, then asks how thresholds, timescales, connection strengths, spatial footprints, and noise reshape persistent working-memory bumps.

The transferable result is crisp: inhibitory subtype structure changes both whether a bump is stable and how it fails. Slow inhibitory timescales can produce either shift-like drift/traveling modes or scale-like oscillatory collapse. Weak inhibitory-to-inhibitory connections can have large and nonmonotonic effects. Broader inhibitory projections expand stable regimes, and broader SST connectivity specifically reduces noise-driven diffusion of memory position. That is a useful computational object for anyone thinking about cognitive-state protection during intervention.

This pairs well with the archive note **Modelling chronic stress as an excitatory-inhibitory perturbation in recurrent working-memory networks**, because both papers refuse vague E/I rhetoric and force the mechanism into a testable perturbation. It also belongs near **Subthalamic stimulation modulates working memory-related cortical dynamics in Parkinson's disease**, which framed DBS cognitive effects as state-dependent cortical dynamics rather than group-average behavior, and near **Cell-Type-Specific Bidirectional Modulation of the Cortico-Thalamo-Cortical Sensory Pathway by tFUS**, which is another reminder that intervention parameters only become useful when mapped onto cell/circuit mechanism.

## Most relevant paper

**Stability and Wandering of Bumps in Neural Fields with Interneuron Subtypes**

This is the most relevant paper today because it gives a concrete computational answer to a common sloppy move: saying "inhibition stabilizes working memory" as though inhibition were a single knob. The paper splits inhibition into PV and SST populations, gives them different spatial and circuit roles, and then asks what happens to persistent activity bumps.

The model is analytically useful. It uses a circular feature space, Heaviside firing rates, exponential coupling kernels, and three coupled populations. Stationary bumps are characterized by threshold-crossing interfaces. Linear stability separates into shifting modes, which correspond to drift or traveling-wave failure, and scaling modes, which correspond to expansion/contraction or collapse. Weak stochastic forcing is then projected onto the translational mode to derive an effective diffusion coefficient for memory wandering.

The key result is that subtype structure changes the control surface. PV and SST thresholds and timescales alter both stability and the kind of instability that appears. Weak inhibitory-to-inhibitory connections can expand a stable region and still reduce the strongest restoring rate, so "more inhibitory coupling" is not a simple stabilizer. Broader inhibitory spatial footprints are different: they expand stable regimes and make the best restoring rates more negative. For SST projections, broader spatial reach also reduces diffusion of bump position under noise.

The caveat is that this is not an empirical result. The parameters are qualitative, the firing-rate nonlinearity is idealized, the model omits VIP and state-dependent synapses, and the analysis is restricted to single-bump, weak-noise regimes. Still, the framing is worth keeping. It makes cell-type specificity relevant to state stability, memory precision, and possible intervention failure modes, which is much better than decorative PV/SST name-dropping.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

- **For computational neuroscience:** interneuron diversity should be tested by whether it changes the stability geometry, not whether it can be named in the introduction.
- **For working-memory models:** delay-dependent error can be framed as diffusion along a neutral attractor mode whose rate depends on circuit architecture.
- **For intervention logic:** shift instability and scale instability imply different control problems; one is drift of a representation, the other is deformation or loss of the represented state.
- **For E/I psychiatry:** the paper pairs with chronic-stress E/I modeling as a reminder that "imbalance" is not a mechanism until the operator is specified.
- **For neuromodulation:** cognitive safety may depend on preserving the right stability margins, not just suppressing or exciting a target region.
- **For CBT-plus-interventional psychiatry:** the new tDCS-plus-CBT pain RCT is worth tracking, but it needs full-text inspection before it becomes a preserved mechanism note.
- **For hypnosis/hypnotherapy:** recent hits were clinically and contextually interesting, especially pain and matching discussions, but none beat today's full-text computational mechanism.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The September fourteenth lesson is that cell-type specificity earns its keep only when it changes the dynamics we can reason about. Today's preserve does not say how to stimulate a patient or treat a symptom. It does something upstream and useful: it shows how PV/SST architecture can change the existence, failure mode, and diffusion of a persistent cognitive state. That is the kind of model detail that can eventually matter for adaptive intervention, because protecting cognition is a stability problem before it is a slogan.
