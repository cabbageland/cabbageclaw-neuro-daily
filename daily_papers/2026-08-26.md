## Theme

## Short overview

The preserve today is **Accelerated DMN-targeted cTBS improves processing speed deficits in schizophrenia**. It wins because it asks a much cleaner question than the average precision-neuromodulation paper. Instead of finding a personalized network target, delivering one dose, and declaring mechanism, it explicitly compares a sham-controlled single-session study against a multi-session accelerated follow-up. The single session does nothing for cognition. The multi-session protocol finally moves a real behavioral outcome.

That design choice matters more than the headline. The paper targets an individualized left posterior inferior parietal default-mode-network node rather than another depression-style left-DLPFC retread. In the first study, ten schizophrenia participants complete randomized crossover visits with intermittent theta-burst stimulation, continuous theta-burst stimulation, and sham. No processing-speed improvement shows up. In the second study, seventeen schizophrenia participants and twelve non-psychosis controls receive five accelerated cTBS sessions in one day, with pre/post resting-state fMRI and MATRICS processing-speed testing. In the schizophrenia group, the composite processing-speed score improves, digit-symbol coding improves more clearly than the other subtests, greater reduction in DMN connectivity tracks greater cognitive improvement, and younger participants improve more than older ones.

That is still not a clean therapeutic proof. The accelerated study has no sham arm, which is the paper's biggest weakness and the reason this note is a strong preserve instead of a triumphalist one. The detailed inspection in this run comes from the accessible PMC-hosted medRxiv preprint, checked against the newer Molecular Psychiatry abstract, not the final journal full text. But even with those limits, the paper earns preservation because it forces the field to separate three claims that are usually blurred together: you can find a network target, you can perturb that network once, and you can improve behavior after enough repeated dose. Those are not the same claim.

The freshest comparison paper today was **Individualized Connectivity-Guided Versus Conventional Targeting of Accelerated Theta-Burst Stimulation in Depression: A Randomized, Double-Blind, Parallel-Design Trial**. It is a serious depression RCT, and the abstract-level story is real: structural-connectivity-guided iTBS beats the 5-centimeter rule early, while by week twelve the between-group differences are no longer significant. But today it stayed a citation rather than a preserve because the inspectable evidence here was still abstract-level, and the repo already has a fuller archived note on a closely related connectivity-guided accelerated depression protocol in a harder inpatient cohort.

The archive keeps the page balanced instead of letting one schizophrenia TMS paper monopolize the logic. **Connectivity-guided accelerated theta burst stimulation as augmentation for inpatient treatment-resistant depression: a randomized, double-blind, sham-controlled trial** remains the stronger fully preserved clinical precision anchor for a sham-controlled depression setting. **A Whole-Brain Dynamical Framework Linking Resting-State Activity to TMS-Evoked Responses** is still the better computational note on how intrinsic dynamics constrain perturbation spread. **Noninvasive brain stimulation combined with evidence-based psychotherapy for psychiatric disorders: A meta-analysis of optimal implementation parameters** remains the more useful standing anchor for CBT-plus-intervention design than today's visible combination papers. And **Effective correction of extreme capacitive artifacts in TMS-EEG via windowed detrending** still matters because network-targeting claims get fake fast when the sensing layer is artifact theater.

The standing-interest checks did not beat the archive. In the **CBT-plus-interventional psychiatry** lane, the visible randomized trial **Efficacy of combined low-frequency rTMS and cognitive behavioral therapy for unmedicated panic disorder** looks real enough, with fifty participants and earlier symptom reduction in the active arm, but it stays narrower and less decision-relevant for this repo than the existing combination-treatment meta-analysis. In the **hypnosis and hypnotherapy** lane, **Mindful Hypnotherapy for Cluster B Personality Disorders: A Randomized Controlled Trial with an Active Control** is a respectable 120-person psychotherapy study, but it is still a symptom-scale outcome paper rather than a stronger hypnosis state, biomarker, or intervention-mechanism note than **Decoding hypnotic consciousness: neural and experiential insights into induced and ideomotor suggestions**.

So the useful August 26 lesson is not that personalized network targeting has arrived. It is more severe than that. A network target only starts to become interesting when the paper can show that repeated stimulation changes behavior, not just connectivity, and when it admits that one well-aimed pulse is not the same thing as an intervention.

## Most relevant paper

**Accelerated DMN-targeted cTBS improves processing speed deficits in schizophrenia**

The paper deserves attention because it refuses one of the field's laziest shortcuts. Many personalized stimulation papers act as if finding a plausible network target and perturbing it once is already enough evidence that the intervention logic is real. This paper instead builds the argument in two steps. First, a randomized sham-controlled single-session crossover study shows that one session is not enough to move processing speed. Then a multi-session accelerated protocol aimed at the same individualized default-mode target finally produces a behavioral effect.

That distinction is exactly why the note is useful. The interesting target is not the usual frontal mood site. It is a participant-specific left posterior inferior parietal node inside the default mode network, chosen because the authors want to reduce DMN hyperconnectivity tied to schizophrenia-related cognitive impairment. In the accelerated study, the schizophrenia group shows improved composite processing speed, stronger digit-symbol improvement than the other subtests, and a coupling between DMN connectivity reduction and behavioral gain. The paper is not saying "the DMN matters." That part was already obvious. It is saying a repeated dose against a personalized DMN node may be able to move the behavior that the network story is supposed to explain.

The caution is non-negotiable. The accelerated study does not have a sham control arm. The total sample is modest. The detailed read in this run comes from the accessible PMC-hosted medRxiv preprint plus the newer Molecular Psychiatry abstract, not the final journal full text. So this is not final proof of an effective cognitive treatment. But it is still preserve-worthy because it makes the right conceptual cut: one-session target engagement, repeated-dose cognitive change, and network-change mediation are different claims and should stay different.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

- **For personalized network targeting:** this paper raises the bar slightly by showing that a target is only interesting once repeated stimulation changes behavior, not just connectivity.
- **For schizophrenia cognition work:** it points at a left posterior inferior parietal default-mode target rather than another frontal symptom target, which is a useful change of intervention vocabulary.
- **For dose logic:** the single-session null result is almost as informative as the accelerated positive result, because it stops the paper from pretending that one well-aimed perturbation should already look therapeutic.
- **For comparison with today's fresh depression RCT:** the new connectivity-guided accelerated iTBS depression paper looks serious, but at abstract level it still reads more like a promising early efficacy citation than a fully inspectable preserve-grade note.
- **For CBT-plus-interventional psychiatry:** today's visible panic-disorder rTMS-plus-CBT trial is real, but it does not beat the archive's broader implementation meta-analysis.
- **For hypnosis and hypnotherapy:** today's visible mindful-hypnotherapy RCT is clinically respectable, but it does not beat the archive's stronger hypnosis state-change and agency note.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The useful August 26 lesson is that personalized network targeting should not get free credit merely for being personalized. A serious paper has to show whether one session changes anything meaningful, whether repeated dose changes behavior, and whether the claimed network marker moves with that behavior. Today's schizophrenia DMN paper still has a major sham-control hole, but it is preserve-worthy because it makes those distinctions explicit instead of collapsing them into precision-neuromodulation marketing.
