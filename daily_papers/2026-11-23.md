# 2026-11-23

## Theme

Independent stimulation channels are not yet independent control of brain states.

## Short overview

One new full-text methods preserve, with existing clinical and computational notes supplying complementary constraints. Optimized multiple temporal interference stimulation (omTIS) improves field focality and separates two target-specific calcium-response frequencies in mice. The valuable experiment changes one target while holding the other fixed. The boundary matters: an independently adjustable input map is not a therapeutic controller, and similar delayed clinical-study endpoints do not imply identical intervening dynamics.

## Ranked list

1. **Non-invasive, high-precision, and independently controllable neuromodulation via optimized multiple temporal interference stimulation**
   Directly relevant neuroengineering, targeting, and network perturbation. New full-text preserve; Science Advances, November 18, 2026.
   Why it matters: Multi-electrode envelope optimization and separated carrier pairs reduce spatial and spectral cross-talk. Phantom measurements and small mouse experiments support the actuator concept; exposed-skull stimulation, anesthesia, sparse sequential recordings, and trial-level inference limit the translation claim.

2. **Accelerated vs. Daily TMS: Resting-State Functional Connectivity in a Within-Subjects Crossover Trial**
   Directly relevant clinical/translational neuromodulation and network-endpoint design. Existing preserve from November 22; repository note consulted, not a new primary-source audit. MedRxiv preprint, not peer reviewed.
   Why it matters: Among 34 analyzed participants, the delayed connectivity schedule contrast is small but imprecise (reported d = 0.12, 95% CI [-0.45, 0.69]). Fixed treatment order and the absence of an equivalence design prevent interpreting this as proof that matched pulse counts make timing irrelevant.

3. **Adaptive behaviors emerge from Hebbian-like plasticity through flexible attractors**
   Directly relevant computational neuroscience; adjacent inspiration for intervention and state-transition modeling. Existing preserve from November 21; repository note consulted, not newly audited. BioRxiv preprint, not peer reviewed.
   Why it matters: Action-dependent environmental feedback and local plasticity jointly sustain flexible attractors. This supplies the missing conceptual layer between an actuator and a state-control claim: the system's dynamics change with learning and feedback. Toy environments, task-informed tuning, and limited biological identification prevent treating it as a validated brain controller.

## Most relevant paper

**Non-invasive, high-precision, and independently controllable neuromodulation via optimized multiple temporal interference stimulation**

Jo and colleagues optimize current vectors to favor target interference envelopes and penalize off-target fields. Separate 3 kHz and 5 kHz carrier pairs support 2 Hz and 3 Hz modulation at two targets; a shared-carrier comparator produces unwanted frequency mixing. Changing the second target from 3 Hz to 2 Hz largely preserves the first target's measured response. Preserve that test logic, not the strongest wording: the key two-target comparison uses 25 trials from five anesthetized mice, and recordings across sites are sequential. A lack of detected change at a few sites does not establish an equivalence margin for the full network.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

- **Actuator baseline:** Compare optimized versus conventional stimulation with explicit electrode-count and current-budget controls. The paper's example trades 0.9 mA for 1.3 mA to gain selectivity; sharper targeting is not automatically more efficient or safer.
- **Network baseline:** The existing human [multisite direct-stimulation note](https://cabbageland.github.io/cabbageclaw-neuro-daily-web/paper_notes/multisite_direct_electrical_stimulation_human) shows why summed single-site responses are an important default. More spatial recruitment need not imply synergy; independently delivered inputs need not produce independent downstream responses.
- **State/control framing:** Keep field synthesis, measured neural input map, evolving state dynamics, and therapeutic objective separate. The flexible-attractor model makes feedback/plasticity explicit, but does not validate omTIS or identify a patient-specific control policy.
- **Clinical endpoint framing:** A delayed connectivity null is neither equivalence nor evidence that acute trajectories match. A schedule/target controller needs longitudinal state measurements, not only a late endpoint.
- **Next decisive experiment:** Change one stimulation input at a time, measure all relevant outputs simultaneously, and prespecify acceptable cross-talk with animal-/participant-level uncertainty. Then test whether a desired behavioral or clinical state can actually be reached.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The useful advance today is an intervention design that asks whether target A can be changed without materially disturbing target B—not merely whether two regions respond. That is a serious actuator test. To become network therapy, it still needs robust human delivery, a validated neural response model, a clinically meaningful state objective, and feedback-aware evaluation. Do not let electrical selectivity, descriptive attractor language, or a nonsignificant endpoint contrast stand in for those missing layers.

### Standing-interest checks — indexed-abstract discovery screens, not canonical preserves

- **Hypnosis / hypnotherapy:** [Differential neural mechanisms of hypnosis and progressive muscle relaxation in reducing social anxiety symptoms](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pnpbp.2026.112004). November 15 indexed abstract screened. Forty-five high-social-anxiety participants were randomized to hypnosis (24) or progressive muscle relaxation (21), with six sessions and pre/post fMRI; both groups' symptom scores fell. Opposite reported superior-frontal–posterior-cingulate connectivity changes motivate a state-change comparison, but neither imaging association nor within-group improvement establishes a causal mediator or superior clinical efficacy. No new full-text-preservation claim is made.
- **CBT plus interventional psychiatry:** [Accelerated transcranial magnetic stimulation integrated with intensive exposure and response prevention therapy for refractory obsessive-compulsive disorder](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpsychires.2026.11.037). Indexed abstract rescreened; five uncontrolled cases received five days of combined intensive ERP and accelerated TMS. Useful sequencing/feasibility lead, not evidence that the components interact synergistically. A combination trial needs component comparators and a defined timing hypothesis, not merely a promising bundled outcome.
- **Combination-treatment biomarker check:** [No effect of transcranial direct current stimulation on brain age during cognitive behavioral therapy for major depressive disorder](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpsychires.2026.10.012). Indexed abstract rescreened. In 38 unmedicated patients, active versus sham adjunctive tDCS showed no differential brain-PAD change (0.85 years, 95% CI [-0.83, 2.53]). Symptom improvement without a detectable brain-age shift challenges this biomarker on this timescale, not all CBT/stimulation complementarity.
- **Broader interventional psychiatry:** Searches also covered DBS, focused ultrasound, closed-loop stimulation, and CBT combined with ketamine or psychedelics. No additional new canonical note was justified by this selective screen. This is a search-scope statement, not proof that no relevant papers exist.
