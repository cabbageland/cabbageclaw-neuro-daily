## Short overview

The new preserve is **High-intensity focused ultrasound (HIFU) and low-intensity focused ultrasound (LIFU) as neurosurgical approaches in psychiatry: A systematic review of current clinical evidence**.

It is worth keeping because it maps how small the human psychiatric focused-ultrasound evidence base still is. The review found only eight eligible studies: three HIFU/MRgFUS studies, mostly anterior-limb-of-internal-capsule capsulotomy for severe OCD or MDD, and five LIFU studies across mood, anxiety, trauma-related, and schizophrenia-negative-symptom populations. The symptom signals are interesting, and the short-term tolerability picture is mostly reassuring, but the methods are too heterogeneous and underpowered to support clinical triumph language.

The digest point is blunt: HIFU and LIFU should not be mashed into one shiny "ultrasound works" claim. HIFU is largely lesion logic for severe refractory illness; LIFU is reversible neuromodulation logic that still needs target-engagement biomarkers, dose-response work, and better sham-controlled trials. The useful standard remains: prove where the acoustic energy goes, prove what state or circuit changes, then ask whether symptoms move durably.

## Most relevant paper

**High-intensity focused ultrasound (HIFU) and low-intensity focused ultrasound (LIFU) as neurosurgical approaches in psychiatry: A systematic review of current clinical evidence**

This is the most relevant paper today because it contextualizes the entire focused-ultrasound psychiatry stack after the recent amygdala and subcallosal-cingulate preserves. It finds 27 HIFU/MRgFUS psychiatric patients across three studies and 138 LIFU participants across five studies. HIFU studies mainly target ALIC with an ablative capsulotomy logic for treatment-refractory OCD or MDD; LIFU studies target fronto-limbic circuitry such as right fronto-temporal cortex, amygdala, and DLPFC with reversible neuromodulation logic.

The value is not that the review proves ultrasound psychiatry. It does not. Its value is that it makes the gap legible: the signal is promising enough to keep studying, but the evidence is still too small, heterogeneous, short-horizon, and bias-exposed to support routine clinical implementation.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

- **For clinical/translational neuromodulation:** today's preserve strengthens the archive's framing that focused ultrasound psychiatry is investigational. The important split is irreversible HIFU lesioning versus reversible LIFU modulation, not generic ultrasound enthusiasm.
- **For focused ultrasound baselines:** the review is a map; the amygdala and subcallosal-cingulate notes remain better individual examples of target-engagement logic. Use the review to orient the field, then use primary studies to judge mechanism.
- **For intervention logic:** symptom-scale movement without acoustic-field accountability and target-engagement measurement should stay low-confidence.

## One-paragraph takeaway

September twenty-sixth is a reality-check day for focused ultrasound psychiatry. The technology is genuinely interesting because it can plausibly reach deep affective and fronto-limbic targets that ordinary noninvasive stimulation mostly reaches indirectly. But the clinical evidence still consists of a small HIFU ablation literature and an early LIFU neuromodulation literature with heterogeneous diagnoses, targets, parameters, and endpoints. The steal-worthy rule is simple: separate lesion logic from modulation logic, and do not let positive symptom scales substitute for target verification.
