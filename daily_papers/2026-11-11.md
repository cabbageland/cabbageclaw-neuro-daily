## Theme

Match the intervention budget before crediting the timing; match the internal state before crediting the biomarker.

## Short overview

One new clinical full-text preserve anchors today's digest: a blinded, randomized-order DBS experiment finds no consistent acute tremor advantage for 185 over 130 Hz when delivered energy is matched. The important result is the comparison design, not a declaration that frequency never matters. Its equivalence analysis is exploratory, uses a ±20-percentage-point margin, and is not uniformly conclusive at the sparsest dose step.

Two existing computational/network notes provide context rather than pretend to be new discoveries. Joint sensing-and-actuation design asks what the controller can actually observe and influence; rhythm-switching models ask whether a successful population readout identifies the circuit mechanism. Together they suggest three distinct gates for stimulation claims: fair dose, feasible interface, and a meaningful endpoint. Today's scout covered clinical/translational work, network/computational neuroscience, methods/neuroengineering, hypnosis/hypnotherapy, and CBT plus interventional psychiatry.

## Most relevant paper

**No Additional Benefit of 185 Hz Versus 130 Hz at Equivalent Energy in Deep Brain Stimulation for Tremor: A Randomized Prospective Clinical Trial**

The study enrolled 18 people with essential tremor and 25 with Parkinson's disease; the primary analysis used 29 ET and 30 PD hemispheres. Frequency-series order was randomized, patients and the examiner were blinded, pulse width was fixed at 60 microseconds, and current was increased through steps permitting equal-energy and equal-current comparisons. Neither cohort showed a significant frequency term or frequency-by-energy interaction in the primary mixed-effects analysis. Side effects appeared at lower currents at 185 Hz, but not at significantly different energy thresholds.

The caveat belongs next to the result: energy matching changes current as well as frequency, so it does not hold neural recruitment constant. Acute response similarity also says little about chronic habituation, individual exceptions, or other targets and symptoms. The original sample-size calculation targeted superiority; the later equivalence analysis cannot turn a negative superiority result into a blanket proof of interchangeability.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

- **Baseline impact:** Compare adaptive or frequency-optimized stimulation against a well-programmed conventional condition at matched resources and tolerability. At fixed current, 185 versus 130 Hz increases modeled delivered energy by about 42%; apparent gains need not demonstrate a timing-specific benefit.
- **Framing:** Distinguish physical electrical dose from spatial recruitment, observable neural state, and symptom outcome. Equal TEED is an informative resource constraint, not a universal measure of biological equivalence or actual battery consumption.
- **Control/modeling:** The latent-control and rhythm-switching notes remain useful precisely because they address different problems: whether an interface can move a state, and whether the measured output identifies that state. Neither simulation supplies missing clinical efficacy evidence.
- **Novelty restraint:** This trial strengthens a practical comparison, not a new mechanism of DBS. Axonal saturation is a discussion-level interpretation here, not an experimentally established explanation.

## One-paragraph takeaway

A controller can appear smarter because it spends more energy, has unrealistic state access, or optimizes a readout that admits the wrong internal solution. Today's clinical paper directly addresses the first problem; the two existing computational notes sharpen the other two. A useful next experiment would compare policies under explicit energy and tolerability constraints, retain patient-level response trajectories, and measure circuit engagement alongside behavior. The aim is not to worship a single budget metric, but to make clear what changed, what was measured, and which causal claim the comparison actually supports.
