## Short overview

The new preserve is **Ictal Epileptogenic Network Differences in Posterior Cingulate Epilepsy Subtypes Based on SEEG**.

This is the right paper to keep today because it turns a slippery localization problem into a concrete network-dynamics question. Posterior cingulate cortex and retrosplenial cortex epilepsies can look clinically similar, but the SEEG propagation pattern is not the same. RSC epilepsy preferentially recruits ipsilateral mesial temporal and hippocampal structures during early seizure spread, while PCC epilepsy looks more diffuse and lacks a stable directional signature in the sampled network.

The value is not that this small retrospective cohort solves posterior cingulate epilepsy. The value is the intervention frame: subtype labels should be tested as patient-specific propagation geometries, especially when the pathway touches memory circuits that matter for surgical planning and cognitive-risk preservation.

## Most relevant paper

**Ictal Epileptogenic Network Differences in Posterior Cingulate Epilepsy Subtypes Based on SEEG**

This is the most relevant paper today because it has unusually direct leverage on intervention logic. The authors study 18 patients with SEEG-confirmed posterior cingulate epilepsy subtypes, at least two recorded habitual seizures, surgical or thermocoagulation treatment, more than one year of follow-up, and Engel I seizure-free outcomes. That outcome anchor matters: the network analysis is not floating over an uncertain localization problem.

The core method is compact. The paper segments seizures into preictal, ictal-onset, and early-propagation windows, estimates gamma-band directed functional connectivity with the nonlinear h2 coefficient, and uses graph metrics plus linear mixed-effects models to compare region-level driver and receiver roles. In RSC epilepsy, the propagation phase shows stronger directed involvement of ipsilateral mesial temporal and hippocampal structures. The lateral temporal cortex behaves more like a receiver. PCC epilepsy does not show the same consistent directional pattern.

The caveat is equally important. This is a retrospective, single-center, 18-patient study with all-male RSC cases and hypothesis-driven electrode coverage. h2 directionality is not causal proof, and the analysis lacks diffusion imaging to connect functional propagation with structural pathways. Still, the paper is worth preserving because it models the right thing: seizure subtypes as directed network dynamics with surgical and cognitive consequences.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

- **For intracranial network neuroscience:** the paper makes the PCC/RSC distinction operational by asking how ictal activity flows, not merely where it starts.
- **For intervention design:** the strongest idea is driver/receiver role separation across seizure phases. That framing transfers cleanly to stimulation, closed-loop control, and surgical planning.
- **For cognitive-risk preservation:** the RSC-to-mesial-temporal pathway gives a plausible bridge between epileptogenic propagation and visual-memory impairment.
- **For target selection:** this is a warning against region-name targeting. PCC and RSC are neighboring posterior midline structures, but their propagation geometries may demand different intervention reasoning.
- **For computational/control framing:** the h2-plus-graph pipeline is simple, but it expresses the right control question: which nodes drive spread, which nodes receive it, and when?
- **For clinical/translational neuromodulation:** individualized accelerated iTBS for treatment-resistant depression, acoustic vagus nerve stimulation, and cervical tcVNS items surfaced today, but accessible full text or preservation value did not beat the SEEG paper.
- **For hypnosis and hypnotherapy:** the standing check did not surface a strong new mechanistic hypnosis or hypnotherapy paper worth preserving today.
- **For CBT plus interventional psychiatry:** ketamine-assisted psychotherapy with subcutaneous esketamine surfaced as relevant context, but the available inspection was abstract-level and the nonrandomized design makes it a context item rather than today's canonical note.

## One-paragraph takeaway

October third is a useful reminder that network neuroscience earns its keep when it changes intervention reasoning. RSC and PCC epilepsy are not just adjacent labels in posterior midline cortex; in this SEEG cohort, RSC seizures preferentially recruit ipsilateral mesial temporal and hippocampal structures during propagation, while PCC seizures are more diffuse. The practical lesson travels beyond epilepsy: localizing a pathological origin is not enough. For surgery, stimulation, and closed-loop control, the next question is how the pathological state moves through the circuit, which nodes become drivers, and which cognitive systems are placed at risk.
