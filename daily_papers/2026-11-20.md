## Theme

A brain-state coordinate system is not yet an intervention model.

## Short overview

One new full-text network-neuroscience preserve: auditory and visual target detection share dominant cortical coordinates but trace different event-associated BOLD trajectories. Its most useful result is methodological restraint—soft Gaussian occupancy is not a set of discrete biological states. Two existing notes supply the missing intervention layers: uncertainty-aware active identification in simulated circuits, and an actual sham-controlled rTMS-plus-CBT trial whose primary clinical outcome was negative. Together they span network neuroscience, computational/control modeling, methods, and clinical/translational neuromodulation without pretending all three offer the same kind of evidence.

## Most relevant paper

**Sensory Change Detection Unfolds Within a Shared Low-Dimensional Brain-State Landscape**

Parvin, Meador, and Razavi fit PLS to group-average auditory/visual connectivity and project run-wise BOLD onto the loadings. Two dominant dimensions are reproducible within participant splits; exploratory event modeling suggests a shared 4-second target-associated displacement followed by modality-dependent 4–6-second evolution. These are hemodynamic response summaries, not neural latencies. The main intervention-relevant lesson is negative but useful: a descriptive landscape neither identifies causal coupling nor tells a stimulator how to move a patient toward a beneficial trajectory.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

- **Representation:** Four PLS dimensions describe 97.1% of the reported symmetric shared cross-modal structure—not 97.1% of BOLD variance. Freeze loadings before independent-subject evaluation rather than treating within-sample map stability as prospective decoding.
- **State claims:** A three-component Gaussian fit is an interpretable coarse-graining. With 57.7% of samples below 0.8 maximum membership probability, preserve posterior uncertainty instead of manufacturing discrete states.
- **Prediction versus intervention:** VAR R² is 0.43 ± 0.16; target timing adds only 0.005 ± 0.015. Compare with simple temporal baselines and forward-chaining validation before using the fitted event coefficient as an input-response model.
- **Causal baseline:** Separate sensory deviance, salience, and button pressing; test actual perturbations. FC hubs and signed loadings are not control nodes or excitation/inhibition.
- **Clinical baseline:** PALDI supplies a simulated identification-to-control test; the gambling trial supplies active-versus-sham augmentation on a common CBT background. Neither validates this new fMRI representation. Clinical synergy also needs more than improvement in both combined-treatment arms.

## One-paragraph takeaway

Keep three layers separate: coordinates that describe activity, dynamics that predict responses to inputs, and outcomes worth changing. Today's new paper contributes mainly the first and a descriptive version of the second. The existing control note shows how to demand an intervention test, while the clinical note shows why even plausible process changes cannot replace a successful primary comparison. The steal-worthy workflow is to validate each bridge rather than let the word “state” carry the whole therapeutic argument.
