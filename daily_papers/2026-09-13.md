## Short overview

The best new preserve today is **Discovering Subtypes of Neurodegenerative Progression with a Scalable Connectome-Constrained Dynamic Model**. It is a full-text arXiv preprint, not a clinical deployment result, but it earns the keep because it forces Parkinson's disease subtype discovery into a trajectory model constrained by brain connectivity. That is much better than another cluster story dressed up as precision medicine.

The model extends COMIND into a subtype-aware disease progression framework. Biomarker abnormality evolves through coupled logistic-diffusion dynamics over a structural connectome, while each subtype gets a sparse forcing pattern and each subject gets a latent disease-time shift. In PPMI, the model uses 85 imaging and clinical biomarkers from 313 Parkinson's subjects, chooses four subtypes by BIC, and reports that those subtypes align with hold-out TD/PIGD motor phenotype and full-cohort genetic groupings while a matched SuStaIn benchmark does not. That is not enough to call the subtypes actionable, but it is a more serious validation target than reconstruction fit alone.

This pairs cleanly with the archive note **Activity-dependent epidemic spreading on multiscale brain networks predicts Alzheimer's disease progression**, which made activity part of the pathology-spread operator, and with **Cohort-amortized personalization: navigating the privacy-utility frontier for virtual brain twins**, which asks how mechanistic brain models become deployable across cohorts. It also belongs near **Stable depression subtypes identified using functional connectome normative deviation models and their response to rTMS**, because both notes ask when a subtype becomes intervention-relevant rather than merely descriptive.

The standing-interest checks were useful but not preserve-displacing. The CBT-plus-interventional psychiatry lane again produced negative evidence: a full-text Frontiers meta-analysis found no reliable incremental benefit from active tDCS over sham when both are paired with cognitive-behavioral interventions for depressive disorders. A new JAD randomized trial similarly reports that cathodal tDCS priming did not improve iTBS antidepressant outcomes over sham in 76 medication-free MDD patients, despite high response rates in both arms. The hypnosis lane surfaced an Iranian randomized trial of ego-strengthening hypnotherapy for depressive outpatients with suicidal thoughts and a Gut commentary on matching IBS patients to gut-directed hypnotherapy; interesting, but not stronger than today's full-text progression-model paper. The interventional psychiatry lane also surfaced a multi-site Brain Stimulation TMS-EEG suicidal-ideation correlate, which is important enough to revisit when full text is accessible.

## Most relevant paper

**Discovering Subtypes of Neurodegenerative Progression with a Scalable Connectome-Constrained Dynamic Model**

This is the most relevant paper today because it attacks a specific kind of precision-medicine laziness. Parkinson's heterogeneity is real, but naming subtypes is not the same thing as understanding progression. COMIND's useful claim is that a subtype should be a different dynamical forcing pattern over a constrained brain network, not just a partition of patients in feature space.

The method is concrete enough to critique. It models biomarker abnormality with a logistic-diffusion ODE, uses a structural connectome plus regional self-propagation, estimates a subject-level disease-time shift, and assigns each subject to the subtype whose trajectory best reconstructs their measurements. It adds sparsity penalties to keep subtype forcing and self-propagation from turning into mush, and it uses a Jensen-Shannon penalty so subtypes do not separate mainly because one group is simply later on the disease-time axis.

The PPMI result is promising in the right limited way. The model selects four subtypes from 126 longitudinal training subjects and then shows significant association with TD/PIGD motor phenotype in 187 held-out cross-sectional subjects. Across the full cohort, subtype assignment also associates with genetic group. SuStaIn, fit under a matched protocol on aggregated biomarkers, does not show the same significant associations. That does not prove COMIND has found true biological disease classes, but it is exactly the kind of external pressure a subtype model should face.

The caveats are real. The connectome is group-level, not patient-specific. The hold-out validation is cross-sectional. Clinical measures are modeled as disconnected nodes, which is understandable but conceptually awkward. Hard subtype assignment will hide mixed or uncertain cases. And no one has shown yet that these subtypes improve DBS selection, medication forecasting, cognitive-risk prediction, or trial enrichment. Still, the representational move is worth keeping: heterogeneity should become a trajectory-and-forcing problem before anyone asks clinicians to believe it.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

- **For progression modeling:** subtype discovery is more useful when it changes the dynamical trajectory, not just the label assigned to a patient.
- **For network neuroscience:** a structural connectome can discipline progression models, but group-connectome constraints should not be mistaken for patient-specific mechanism.
- **For computational psychiatry and neurology:** latent disease time and subtype membership need to be separated; otherwise subtypes become disguised staging bins.
- **For personalized intervention:** sparse subtype forcing maps are not control inputs yet, but they are a cleaner place to imagine controllable disease-specific levers.
- **For baseline choice:** the SuStaIn comparison matters because it tests against a recognized subtype-and-stage model rather than an easy clustering straw man.
- **For rTMS/psychiatry stratification:** today's paper reinforces the same standard as the depression connectome-subtype archive note: subtypes earn trust when they predict something clinically external.
- **For CBT-plus-interventional psychiatry:** fresh tDCS-combination evidence remains mostly negative, which is a useful guardrail against lazy "stimulation plus therapy must synergize" claims.
- **For hypnosis/hypnotherapy:** recent hits were clinically interesting but mostly small, commentary-level, or mechanism-light; no hypnosis paper displaced the computational preserve today.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The September thirteenth lesson is that heterogeneity is only useful when it changes the model of how a patient moves through state space. Today's preserve does not hand us actionable Parkinson's subtypes, and it should not be inflated into that. What it does give is a cleaner standard: if a subtype is going to matter for treatment, it should correspond to a different trajectory, source pattern, or controllable vulnerability, and it should be tested against external clinical or biological structure instead of admired as a pretty clustering result.
