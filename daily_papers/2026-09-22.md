## Short overview

The three new preserves are **Longitudinal brain dynamics and prediction of craving changes in individuals with methamphetamine use disorder**, **Time-resolved aperiodic dynamics in event segmentation in attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder**, and **Neural correlates of response inhibition following cognitive behavioral therapy in obsessive-compulsive disorder: a randomized controlled EEG study**.

The MUD paper is the most intervention-adjacent computational preserve. It uses resting-state fMRI, pairwise maximum entropy modeling, energy landscapes, and an RBF support-vector regression model to show that people with methamphetamine use disorder occupy a more rigid brain-state landscape early in abstinence, partially recover flexibility after longer abstinence, and carry baseline dynamic features that predict later craving change. The prediction result is small and unvalidated, but the state-flexibility framing is useful.

The ADHD paper is the developmental/network preserve. Adolescents with ADHD and neurotypical peers watched a continuous film while EEG was recorded and they marked perceived event boundaries. ADHD participants were less sensitive to contextual changes and showed aperiodic EEG dynamics consistent with excessive pre-boundary stabilization and weaker post-boundary re-engagement. This is not treatment evidence, but it turns "temporal disorganization" into a measurable state-transition problem.

The OCD CBT paper is the standing-interest psychotherapy-mechanism preserve. A 12-week CBT-with-ERP program beat waitlist on Y-BOCS, obsessive beliefs, intolerance of uncertainty, No-Go commission errors, and frontocentral No-Go P3 amplitude. The caveat is large: waitlist control, unblinded clinical assessment, low-density EEG, stable SSRI use in roughly half the sample, no long-term follow-up, and no mediation test. Still, it is a useful example of treating psychotherapy as an intervention on measurable control processes.

## Most relevant paper

**Longitudinal brain dynamics and prediction of craving changes in individuals with methamphetamine use disorder**

This is the most relevant paper today because it gives cabbageland a concrete way to talk about recovery as movement through a state landscape. The key claim is not "fMRI predicts craving" in the lazy biomarker sense. The better claim is that early-abstinence MUD participants appear over-confined to a small number of dominant brain states, that this rigidity partially loosens with prolonged abstinence, and that the baseline dwell/transition profile contains some information about later craving change.

The useful transferable object is the process variable: flexible access to minor or alternative network states. If an intervention claims to improve craving regulation, compulsivity, attention, or mood, it should eventually show whether it changes the patient's accessible state space, not just whether a symptom score moved.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

- **For addiction modeling:** craving-recovery prediction should be tested against dynamic-state baselines, not only static connectivity or clinical covariates.
- **For network neuroscience:** dwell time, basin dominance, and direct-versus-indirect transitions are more intervention-useful than another global statement that connectivity differs.
- **For adolescent psychiatry:** the ADHD event-segmentation paper reframes temporal disorganization as a failure of boundary-state updating, which is more actionable than generic inattention.
- **For CBT-plus-interventional psychiatry:** the OCD CBT paper suggests a clean combination-therapy question: does the biological intervention improve the process that therapy actually trains, such as response inhibition or tolerance of uncertainty?
- **For neuromodulation:** the abstract-only adaptive DBS pilot surfaced today but did not displace the full-text preserves. It remains important because chronic adaptive-vs-conventional DBS trials are the right clinical design direction.
- **For sparse sensing:** today's papers lean on expensive or lab-bound measurement; the existing SHRED note remains the practical reminder that state estimation has to survive sparse sensors before it becomes a real intervention loop.
- **For hypnosis/hypnotherapy:** no stronger new full-text hypnosis or hypnotherapy paper displaced today's state-dynamics and CBT-mechanism notes. The standing frame remains state-change measurement plus response verification.

## One-paragraph takeaway

September twenty-second is about what counts as a useful process variable. The MUD paper says craving recovery may depend on loosening a rigid network-state landscape. The ADHD paper says event-boundary updating may fail because the brain over-stabilizes the old state and under-re-engages after the transition. The OCD CBT paper says therapy can be paired with inhibition-related behavior and EEG, but waitlist control and low-density physiology are not enough to crown a mechanism. The shared standard is simple: name the state, measure the transition, test whether the intervention changes it, and do not confuse a correlated readout with a causal engine.
