## Short overview

The best new preserve today is **Sources of the N15 TMS-evoked potential following motor cortex stimulation localize rostrals to TMS-induced electric fields and depend on dose**. It is a full-text Imaging Neuroscience paper available through PMC, and it earns the note because it tests a deceptively basic assumption in TMS-EEG: whether an early evoked component actually sits where the modeled electric field peaks.

The answer is mostly no. In 16 healthy adults receiving left-M1 TMS across six dose levels and two motor states, the electric field peaks around the crowns of the pre- and postcentral gyri, while the N15 source field localizes more rostrally in premotor and motor regions. Higher dose increases N15 magnitude and reduces the mismatch, but the result still points away from a simple local-readout interpretation. N15 looks more like a fast field-to-circuit consequence than a direct measurement of the maximally exposed cortical patch.

This pairs well with **A coupling model of transcranial magnetic stimulation induced electric fields to neural state variables**, which argues that field-to-state coupling is a missing mechanistic layer in TMS modeling, and with **Depression Improvement Correlates With Lower TMS Intensity in a Randomized Trial With Real-Time E-Field Modeling**, which treats clinical dose as modeled cortical field delivery instead of motor-threshold folklore. It also belongs beside **A Whole-Brain Dynamical Framework Linking Resting-State Activity to TMS-Evoked Responses**, because both papers make the same larger point: perturbation readouts need a circuit model, not just a target coordinate.

## Most relevant paper

**Sources of the N15 TMS-evoked potential following motor cortex stimulation localize rostrals to TMS-induced electric fields and depend on dose**

This is the most relevant paper today because it turns a vague target-engagement assumption into an actual spatial test. The usual TMS-EEG temptation is to see an early TEP and treat it as evidence that the stimulated cortical patch responded. This paper asks whether that interpretation survives direct comparison with individualized electric-field maps.

The method is concrete. Sixteen healthy adults received TMS over the left primary motor hand area at intensities from `rMT - 8% MSO` to `rMT + 12% MSO`, during rest and tonic contraction. The authors used T1/T2 MRI-based head models to estimate the electric field, localized the N15 source with LCMV, dSPM, and eLORETA, and tested dose/state effects with mixed models and spatial clustering.

The result is a warning: N15 is not just the local field maximum speaking back. The modeled electric field peaks around pre- and postcentral gyral crowns, while N15 source activity is more rostral, mainly premotor and motor. Higher dose increases N15 magnitude and brings source activity closer to the field distribution, but the mismatch remains meaningful enough to support a propagation interpretation, possibly through cortico-cortical or cortico-subcortico-cortical loops.

The caveats are the right ones. Electric-field magnitude is not neuronal activation; source localization has no intracranial ground truth here; nine participants needed interpolated coil coordinates; and TMS-EEG artifacts always deserve suspicion. Still, the preserve is useful precisely because it does not overclaim. It says: if you want TMS-EEG to be a target-engagement readout, prove what the readout is coupled to.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

- **For TMS-EEG biomarkers:** early does not automatically mean local. N15 may be an early circuit response rather than a direct map of the field peak.
- **For targeting:** target coordinates, E-field peaks, effective neural activation, and source-localized responses are separate objects that need to be related empirically.
- **For dose logic:** higher intensity changes both magnitude and apparent source geometry, so dose can alter what physiological object a biomarker reflects.
- **For source modeling:** agreement across inverse solvers is helpful, but shared forward-model assumptions can still create shared bias.
- **For adaptive stimulation:** a closed-loop variable should be labeled local, propagated, or mixed before anyone lets it drive a controller.
- **For CBT-plus-interventional psychiatry:** today's clinical combination hits did not add a stronger mechanistic result; the field still needs sharper combination logic, not just more pairing.
- **For hypnosis/hypnotherapy:** recent hypnosis items remain clinically interesting but mechanism-light relative to today's field-to-circuit measurement result.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The September sixteenth lesson is that target engagement needs a measurement model. Today's preserve does not say N15 is useless; it says N15 is not allowed to cosplay as a direct local activation readout without proof. For cabbageland, that is the right kind of skepticism: stimulation dose, field modeling, source localization, and state estimation only become intervention logic when we know how the measurement is coupled to the circuit.
