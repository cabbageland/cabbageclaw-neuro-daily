# 2026-11-15

## Theme

More network engagement is not the same thing as synergy—or better treatment.

## Short overview

One new full-text preserve: simultaneous stimulation of two sites in 20 people undergoing epilepsy stereo-EEG monitoring usually produces responses predictable from the sum of single-site responses. Additional recording sites cross the response threshold, mostly without requiring nonlinear amplification. The interesting exceptions depend on location and response timing, not a universal multisite advantage.

Two existing notes supply clinical and computational context: a tiny depression-DBS interaction study asks the composition question in a disease-relevant network; a simulation study makes the sensing/actuation interface explicit. Together they separate four gates: response composition, useful target engagement, realizable control, and therapeutic benefit. This is one new human network/neuroengineering paper, not three new clinical findings.

## Most relevant paper

**Electrophysiology of multisite direct electrical stimulation in the human brain**

Sehgal and colleagues compare interleaved single-site and synchronous dual-site pulses across 87 stimulation pairs in 20 adults with drug-resistant epilepsy, with three-site tests in seven participants. At 7 mA per site, the summed single-site response predicts response presence/absence in 86.7% of recording channels. Almost all additionally responsive evoked-potential channels are classified as linear: a threshold crossing is not itself evidence of synergistic network computation.

Preserve the decomposition, not a therapeutic claim. Richer mixed-effects models barely improve held-out-participant amplitude/AUC prediction over the additive baseline, and peak timing remains poorly predicted. The study deliberately favors pairs with convergent responses; its many channel/pair observations are not independent patients. Lower per-site current, wider recruitment, lower total resource use, and clinical improvement remain separate hypotheses.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

- **Network neuroscience:** Compare measured simultaneous responses with the sum of separate responses before invoking emergent multisite synergy. Keep response detection separate from waveform interaction and desired state change.
- **Methods/neuroengineering:** Report local versus distant effects, early versus late response windows, and the artifact-limited interval. Late supralinearity is compatible with network interactions; it does not identify inhibitory cell mechanisms directly.
- **Computational/control:** Use the additive input-response model as a serious held-out-participant baseline. Better in-sample information criteria are not evidence of better generalization, and pulse-response fitting is not a validated feedback controller.
- **Clinical/translational:** Match total charge, energy, peak current, and relevant safety limits explicitly. Two 4-mA sites versus one 7-mA site is not an automatically dose-matched comparison. Test trains, behavior, and symptoms before translating broader recruitment into treatment superiority.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The useful progression is not “one target was good, so two must be better.” Measure each input separately, predict their combination, locate reproducible residual interactions, then ask whether those interactions move a clinically relevant state under a fair resource budget. This week's human single-pulse study and the existing depression-DBS note both make composition an empirical question, but different cohorts, waveforms, and readouts prevent treating them as a pooled result. A reliable additive model may be more useful for intervention design than a fragile synergy story.
